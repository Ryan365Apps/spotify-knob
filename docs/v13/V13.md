## What v13 changes against v12, and why

1. **The encoder faces up at the crown.** The AEDR-8300 is a reflective sensor Broadcom specifies against a flat codewheel at a 2 mm gap; reading a strip on the curved bore was our adaptation, and it cost height because the strip had to sit above the bell band's contact zone and the wheels' groove above the strip. Now the sensor lies flat on a 0.9 mm boss on the seat flange's top face at 310°, r 76 (between the wheel notch at 270° and the locating nub at 330°), its optical face at 29.53, and reads a code ring in a 0.15 mm recess in the crown's underside at r 72.5–79.5 (the lens overhang ends at r 70, the bore at 83.2) — a stuck film ring on the printed prototype, laser-ablated stripes in the black anodise on the machined knob, no strip at all. 478 mm round, about 6,000 lines at the sensor's 0.08 mm pitch (the bore strip had 5,700). The gap now varies with the knob's axial play on the V-wheels, which the groove sets and which is smaller than the bore's radial run-out on three eccentric bushes. The board (8 × 12, ASSUMED) is held by two M2 × 4 into inserts through the boss and the flange; its leads run inward over the flange. Gone: the tower, its window through the wall, the crush-rib slot through the seat, the lead notch, the code-band recess in the bore. The checker measures the gap (2.00), confirms the sensor's footprint (r 74.1–78.0) lies inside the ring, and that the sensor's reflective keep-out reaches the ring's face and no further.
2. **The bell band goes back to v11's width** (9.0–17.8, 2.9 mm of bore contact above the skirt's bottom at 14.9): v12 had narrowed it to 2.0 only so the code band could drop. Question 21 closes.
3. **The wheels drop to the drive band and the seat is set by the adapter.** The groove sits 2.1 above the band (19.9–23.1), which would allow a seat at 24.2; the adapter's HDMI socket (23.68) needs 26.0, so the seat is 26.0 — 2.0 below v12. Knob top 34.38, overall 35.9. Everything above the seat is the v12 chain (tape 0.5, glass 1.98, lens 2.5, gap 0.4, crown 3.0). The vent slits move to 18.5–23.0 (101 of them, 4.5 tall). The knob is 2.0 shorter again: 69 cm³, 88 g in PETG.
4. **What would come next, and is not built**: with the adapter flipped (socket hanging down) the ceiling becomes the touch board at 22.4 and the seat 24.8 — 1.2 more, for a downward-facing cable entry (question 19). Below 24.2 the wheels themselves are the limit: the drive band's contact zone starts at the knob skirt's bottom, which the halo's lip sets — a narrower strip or a lower motor, a different discussion. The production build with the Compute Module (no adapter) lands on the wheels' floor: seat 24.2, overall 34.1.

Carried from v12 (`docs/v12/V12.md` has the reasoning): the deck-less layout, the Pi on the closing plate's lands with the adapter kit on its standoffs, the connect board on its bracket, the boards' positions, the plate's two air circuits, the up-angled USB-C lead, the ribbon on HDMI1.

Carried unchanged from v11: the Ø175.4 knob with its 20 mm rim and the derived radii; the heatsink plate's construction; the display bonded to the seat; the halo strip on the wall; the port face with its rail and the turned-over jack; the print review's features; the engine-turned edge.

1. **The Pi 5 without its fan, now with a board 1.2 mm over its fins**: log the SoC temperature for an hour of the real workload. The adapter blankets the heatsink; the fins' air leaves sideways under it. If it throttles: 15 mm standoffs (the seat allows the socket 2.3 more), or option B (Pi inverted on a plate boss).
2. **The adapter kit's connect board**: measure it (the model has 30 × 46 × 1.2 from a photograph, corner holes assumed) and confirm the panel's 45-way flex mates with its CN2. The bracket's bosses move to its real holes.
3. **The Pi's USB-C lead**: an up-angled plug no taller than 6.5 with the lead leaving its far end.
4. **The code ring under the crown**: stray daylight through the 0.4 mm rim gap at the lens edge reaches the same cavity the sensor looks into (the bore arrangement had the same exposure through the wall's window); prove it on the bench with the lens fitted, and measure the gap variation as the knob is pushed and pulled on its wheels.
