# the 60 — version 16

Built 8 September 2026. v16 is v15 (`docs/v15/`) plus **the extra 5 mm of radius** (Ryan, 8 Sep: "add the extra 5mm and go ahead with v16"), **the layout study** (`docs/LAYOUT-STUDY.md`: the Pi powered through its header, the bought breakouts on the plate, the microcontroller on a tray), **the halo as HALO-OPTICS.md** (the strip face down at a white floor, a 4 mm machined opal ring standing 2 proud), **the wheel decision of 8 Sep** (bought V-groove bearings on fixed pins, the third on a spring-loaded block in a channel — no eccentric bushes), **the fixings rule** (every board on countersunk M2 screws pointing up, nuts above, printed spacers) and **the small-parts sourcing** (`docs/SMALL-PARTS-SOURCING.md`: the panel jack, the Adafruit light-sensor breakout, the Mill-Max spring-pin block, the MT6701 module, the Pololu converter). The two plate ribs are deleted. **Height unchanged at 35.9 (knob top 34.4); object Ø185.4, Ø189.4 at the halo band.** 0 check failures in both clutch states and in the knob-fitting sequence; A3 sheets for every made part; 14 printed parts (one a family) + the three-piece plate + the machined opal ring.

Three things were found while building that the study and the halo document did not know. They are stated up front because each changed the design:

1. **The wall could only grow 3.6 mm, not 5.** The halo channel must be 4.4 wide (a 4 mm strip face down) inside a 3.9 mm opal ring whose outer face stands 2 mm proud of the knob — that fixes the wall's outer face at r 86.4, its inner at 84.8 (v15 81.2). The layout study assumed +5 for everything rim-relative. The plate boards still fit, at smaller margins than the study reported (3.1–3.3 mm for the motor driver, 1.5 for the level shifter), and the level shifter is on the plate, not on the tray.
2. **The motor's bell would have crossed the halo channel.** At the Ø176.4 bore a Ø35 bell touching the bore reaches r 88.8 over z 8.5–18.2 — straight through the channel at r 86.4–90.8, z 8–13.5. v15's strip sat inside the bell's reach; a face-down channel cannot. So the bell no longer touches the bore: a printed **drive collar** (ID 35 pressed on the bell's top 3.5 mm, OD 39.4) carries the band and meets the bore over z 14.9–17.8, above the ledge; the bell stops at r 86.0, inside the wall's outer face and 0.5 inside the channel's liner. Ratio 4.48 (v15 4.75).
3. **The sprung block cannot be released from above.** The crown covers everything at r > 64.2 once the knob is on, so "put the bezel on and let go" needs a release that is reached from the display opening. The block carries a captive M2 × 18 screw from the centre side: wound in, it pulls the block 4.0 toward the centre against its spring; backed off, the spring pushes the block home and the thread stays in the block's nut at every position. The checker walks an L-key to it.

## What v16 changes against v15, and why

1. **+5 mm** (Ryan, 8 Sep, after the layout study). The display is fixed, so the whole 5 goes into the rim: 25 flat (RULING 6 Sep said at least 20). Bore Ø176.4, knob wall 4.5 as before. 82 knurl starts keep the 7.1 mm pitch.
2. **Bought V-groove bearings on a bore ridge** (Ryan: aluminium bought wheels, "V-groove wheels are good"). The ridge is derived from the bearing's V: crest = root flat + 2 × 0.2, height = the V's depth, so the same 90° flanks slide into each other with 0.2 between crest and root; the bearings' Ø12 rims run 0.2 inside a 0.3 relief band cut behind the ridge and never touch the bore. Everything follows from `WHEEL_V_D` and `WHEEL_V_ROOT_FLAT` — when Ryan's listing is measured, two numbers change (question 36). Two fixed posts carry Ø3 × 10 pins pressed 5.5 deep with an M3 × 0.5 washer under the inner ring; the sprung block carries a Ø3 × 16 pin up through a 3.2 slot in the channel's roof (the slot's ends are the block's stops, 4.0 in / 0.5 out).
3. **The knob-fitting sequence, and the wall's step.** With two fixed wheels there is no way to drop a ridged bore over them straight; the knob goes on 2.4 mm off-centre toward az 90 (each fixed wheel is 60° off that line, so the ridge moves 1.2 outward at both — 0.2 past their rims), descends to its final height, and is slid to centre with the ridge entering the grooves flank on flank. On the far side the ridge's crest then comes in to r 84.9 and the bore to 85.8, so the wall above the halo ledge steps inward 2.0 (a 50° ramp at z 13.8–16.2, printable inverted) and the seat flange ends at 84.4. The checker performs the drop at five heights and the slide at three offsets with the block parked, and confirms the parked wheel (r 84.3) clears the offset crest by 0.6.
4. **The sprung block.** A 13 × 8 × 5 printed block on the plate top in a printed channel (walls 2, roof 0.8 with the pin slot, an end wall at r 58–60 for the spring) — the channel is part of the structure. The spring (ASSUMED Ø4 × 16 free, 0.8 N/mm) runs on the M2 release screw between the end wall and the block's face: 4.0 N seated. The band slip limit at 4 N × 0.2 × r 88 is about 70 mN·m per the earlier estimate's method; question 38 asks the bench whether the detent's 60–150 mN·m target needs more spring, and the block takes a stiffer one without a change.
5. **The halo** as HALO-OPTICS, at the new radius, plus the three things it left open: the shelf (0.8 thick at z 6.7–7.5, 0.35 above the openings' chamfered tops — it does not shadow them); the white floor as a removable printed liner (0.4 floor, 0.6 wall to z 11.4; a white coating on the ring gives 4.0 of throw instead of 3.6 — bench); and the strip's two feeds, both through notches in the wall's foot: the tail at az 325 to the level shifter, the far end's 5 V at az 190 to the converter (question 32 closed: nothing passes through a board).
6. **The drive collar** (item 2 above). PETG printed for the prototype, POM turned in production; a 0.3 lead-in; 0.05 interference on the bell. The band on it at z 14.9–17.8 meets the bore over its full height (the skirt's bottom is 14.9). The bell's window in the wall is 1.0 clear of the bell and 2.0 clear of the collar, open up to the flange for the inverted print; the liner's wall passes across the window 0.5 from the bell, so the cavity is still closed to the channel's light.
7. **Boards and fixings** (the study; FUNCTION-ALLOCATION; Ryan's rule). The plate boards sit on 3.0 printed spacers on M2 countersunk studs whose heads are 0.2 proud under the closing plate (the core stands 1.5 off the desk); each stud rises through a full-depth Ø7 island in the duct, the channels cross-linked round it; nuts above the boards. Positions were re-solved with the block channel, the LRA clip and the HDMI ribbon in the picture (the study had none of them), and with the plate's own constraint that every island stays inside r 73.3, where the intake tunnels begin — the lands between tunnels are too narrow for an island. The audio board's hole pattern (ASSUMED, the board is not laid out) is what keeps its outer studs at r 69.5. The MCU tray is a 40 × 30 × 1.2 plate on the audio board's four studs (4.4 tube spacers above the audio's nuts), the MCU across it between the nuts in a 0.6 pocket, a 1.2 keeper frame clamped by the top nuts holding it by its edges; the USB-C faces inboard.
8. **The Pi through its header**; the HDMI ribbon lifted to z 16.4 (0.3 under the A plug's body) because the driver breakout sits where it lay; the level shifter's data from the Pi's header.
9. **Small parts** as SMALL-PARTS-SOURCING: the port face grows to 56 wide for the 16.5 mm light-sensor breakout (a 5.3 mm slot holder behind the face, the sensor's 6.8 × 2.4 × 3.0 package in a pocket at the hole); the exhaust arcs and the two end ring screws (22.5 → 26, 337.5 → 334) move with the wider slot; the panel jack's bushing in a 3 mm boss; the Mill-Max block on its carrier in the v15 clip; the MT6701 module's 17.4 × 15.4 pocket in the carriage.
10. **Deleted**: the two ribs (Ryan: negligible, and they stop the plate being flipped), the motion board and its washers, the eccentric bushes and collars, the Pi's USB-C plug and notch, the jack and light boards, the 12 × 12 commutation board.
