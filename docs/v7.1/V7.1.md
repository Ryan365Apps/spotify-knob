# the 60 — version 7.1

**What changed.** The knob is one piece. v7 had the crown screwed onto the skirt with six M2s because the Ø115 display was trapped between the crown lip above it and the wheel ridge and peg flange below it. v7.1 removes the trap instead of splitting the knob: the bore is straight (Ø116) so the display passes through from below, the three support wheels ride in a V-*groove* cut into that bore (a groove doesn't block the disc; a ridge did), and each wheel sits on an eccentric bush so it can be pulled out of the groove for assembly and locked back in. No screws, seams or inserts anywhere on the knob.

**Headline numbers.** Ø125 across the knob (was 123), 34.3 mm tall including the pad, knob 23.2 of that (71 % of the side). Picture Ø87.6, lip in to Ø89.0. Full 360° halo, cable tunnel under it at 90°. The fit check finds 0 collisions in both detent states and at four knob angles; the wheels clear the bore by 0.57 mm when retracted; the disc passes the bore with 0.5 mm radial clearance.

**Weight.** Made parts about 321 g (steel plate 214 g, knurled knob 56 g in PETG / 118 g in aluminium). v7 was 313 g; the difference is the wider plate and the taller one-piece knob wall. Not a concern, and the knob's mass is at the rim where it helps the flywheel feel.

## How it goes together (this is the sequence the design assumes)

1. **Base shell first, upside down.** Ring B in (it sits higher), then ring A; their towers pass up through the six ledge slots. Rocker on its pin at 255°, pins into ring A's slot and ring B's tab. Gearmotor + worm at 350° and the drive arm with its motor and tyre hub at 180° sit on the pad in their pockets, so they go in when the plate does.
2. **Wheels.** Drop a bush into each wheel post from above (flange on the post top), bearing + collar onto the pin. Turn each bush with a 2 mm hex key from underneath so the pin points **inward** — wheels retracted.
3. **Display.** Screw the disc to its four posts from below (M2.5, heads under the ledge).
4. **Knob.** Lower it over the display and the retracted wheels; the straight bore clears everything.
5. **Seat the wheels.** From underneath, turn each bush half a turn so the pin points outward; the collar's V enters the groove and the knob is located. Lock the bushes (drop of thread-locker on the prototype; the drawing shows an M2 grub from the inside for production).
6. **Halo and plate.** LED board outside the LED wall, diffuser over it; port board and motors into the plate pockets; plate on with four M3 countersunk screws from below. The plate is last, which is why the bushes can be reached.

## What moved to make room

The ledge, the tower pockets and the encoder tower moved outward 1.9 mm to follow the bore; the towers narrow below the ledge so they pass inside the LED board. The drive tyre went from Ø47 to Ø51 so the motor stays where it was (inside the magnet rings). The pegs are Ø3 balls pressed 2.8 deep, 0.2 proud of the bore; the magnet-to-peg gap is unchanged from v7 (0.4 lip + 0.4 air), so the detent research still applies. The wall under the knurl root at the peg holes is 0.7 mm on the PETG prototype (1.0 mm knurl) and 1.4 mm on the CNC part (0.3 mm knurl).

## Things to verify on the bench

1. **Eccentric lock.** The bushes hold the knob's preload; if a printed bush creeps, that shows up as play in the knob within a day. Brass bushes with a grub-screw lock are the production answer.
2. **Groove flank contact.** The collar's V is modelled 0.05 mm off the groove flanks; the eccentric takes up the rest. If the knob feels notchy at three points per turn, the collars are bottoming on the apex flat — take 0.1 off the collar's OD.
3. **Display board** standoff, component height and hole PCD (unchanged assumptions from v7; the inner roof clears the components by 0.6).
4. **Drive motor envelope** Ø16 × 7, and the **Ø8 gearmotor + worm torque** to turn ring A — neither is sized here; measure on the printed rings.
5. **Supercap bank** (2× Ø8 × 16 assumed), **O-ring grip** (0.15 press), **encoder index strip**, **magnet matching** — as v7.
