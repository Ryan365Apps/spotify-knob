# the 60 — version 7

**What this is.** Version 7 is the first build you can print and put together. Every made part exists as a STEP and an STL, the whole assembly exists as one STEP in both detent states, and every made part has its own ISO-style A3 drawing. Bought parts (display, bearings, motors, magnets, boards) are drawn only as envelopes so the printed parts can be checked against them.

**Headline numbers.** Ø123 across the knob, 34.3 mm tall including the rubber pad (the knob is 23.2 of that, 71 % of the side). The picture is Ø87.6, the crown reaches in to Ø89.0. The full halo goes round 360°; the cable tunnel and the jack sit under it at 90°. The fit check finds 0 collisions in both detent states (clicks on / clicks off) and with the knob turned to 0°, 3°, 17° and 45°.

The knob body is exported **smooth** (no knurl) so the STL is small and prints cleanly for a fit check. The knurled body is the same part with the diamond pattern cut in; it is cached in the source folder and switches on with `knurl=True`.

## How it goes together

1. **Plate first.** Bond the rubber pad under the steel plate. Drop the port board (USB-C, hub, DAC, jack) into its pocket at the back and the two motors into their pockets: the Ø8 planetary gearmotor with its worm at 350°, the flat drive motor (in its tyre hub, on the drive arm) in the Ø19 well at 180°.
2. **Rings under the shell.** Turn the shell over. Ring B goes in first (it sits higher, z 6.2–7.3), then ring A (z 4.8–6.0). Their towers pass up through the six slots in the ledge. Load each 3 × 3 × 6 magnet into its tower from the top before the ring goes in, 6 mm axis vertical, magnetised radially outward, all six the same polarity. Fit the rocker on its Ø2.8 pin at 255° with its pins in ring A's slot and ring B's tab.
3. **Shell onto the plate.** Four M3 countersunk screws from below into the shell's bosses (PCD 75, at 20°/130°/220°/300°). The LED ring board sits outside the LED wall; the diffuser drops over it.
4. **Wheels.** Press a Ø3 × 9 dowel into each wheel post (0°/120°/240°), then a 623ZZ with its V-collar onto each dowel.
5. **Knob.** Lower the knob body over the base; the three wheels engage the V-ridge inside the skirt and set the 0.4 mm running gap. Press the 60 Ø3 chrome-steel balls into their radial holes flush with the bore (do this before the knob goes on).
6. **Display and crown.** The Ø115 display disc drops in from the top onto its four posts (M2.5 from below, heads under the ledge). Screw the crown on from below through the skirt wall, six M2 × 22 at 3° + n·60°.

## What changed from v6c

- **Diameter.** Ø135 was never a requirement; v7 is Ø123. The crown reaches in over the display's dead border to Ø89.0 (picture Ø87.6), so the rim is 17 mm wide instead of 24.
- **Full halo.** The LED wall and the opal ring go round 360°. The cable tunnel is under the halo at 90°, 34 mm wide, roof at z 6; the jack is sink-mounted on the port board with its axis at z 2.2.
- **Detent on/off by turning, not lifting.** The research showed a 3 mm vertical lift leaves 52 % of the click. v7 uses two thin rings under the ledge, each with three magnet towers. Clicks on: all six towers at peg pitch. Clicks off: ring A turns +1.5°, ring B turns −1.5°, so the two groups sit half a peg apart and their pulls cancel. One Ø8 gearmotor with a worm turns ring A; the rocker turns ring B the other way. The move is 1.44 mm at the tower.
- **Self-turning knob.** A flat gimbal-class motor (Ø16 × 7) with a Ø47 O-ring tyre on a pivoting arm. Idle it sits 1.0 mm off the knob's bore; a Ø6 solenoid at 205° swings it in, pressing the tyre 0.15 mm into the bore (ratio 2.38:1). It can drive full rotations, not just nudges.
- **Bearings.** Three 623ZZ bearings in printed V-collars on Ø3 dowels replace the V-groove wheels from the BOM. The knob's ridge and the collars are both 90° V; the knob is located radially and axially by the same three contacts.
- **Ballast.** The 3 mm steel plate is the mass; no hidden mass ring.

## Things to verify on the bench (marked V on the drawings)

1. **Display board.** Standoff height (1 mm assumed), component height under the board (6.4 assumed), hole PCD (104 assumed) and whether the USB-A overhangs. The inner roof clears the components by 0.6 on those numbers.
2. **Drive motor envelope.** Ø16 × 7 is a typical gimbal motor; the well and the arm are cut for that. Measure the one you buy.
3. **Ø8 gearmotor + worm torque.** The worm has to turn ring A against the magnets' side-pull on the pegs and the rocker's friction. Nothing in v7 sizes that torque; measure it on the printed rings before buying the motor.
4. **Supercap bank.** Two Ø8 × 16 radial envelopes at 105°/119°. If the real bank is Ø10 × 30 it needs its own pocket in the plate.
5. **O-ring grip.** 0.15 mm press on a 2 mm nitrile O-ring; if it slips, go to 0.25 or a Shore-40 ring.
6. **Printed pivot pins.** The rocker and drive-arm pivots are printed Ø2.8. Replace with steel dowels if they shear.
7. **Encoder index.** The code band (z 17.4–18.4) needs a bonded reflective strip with one index mark; the AEDR-8300 face is 2.0 from it.
8. **Magnet matching.** The cancel in the clicks-off state depends on the six magnets pulling alike; sort them by pull before fitting.

## Print notes

Print everything in PETG, 0.2 mm layers (0.12 for the collars and the tyre hub). Orientation per part is in `parts/README.txt`. The knob body prints flange-down, the crown prints top-face-down, the shell prints upright on its plinth. Nothing needs supports. The general tolerance on the drawings is ISO 2768-mK; the fits that matter are the 0.4 mm running gap between the knob and the ledge, the 0.05 concentricity of the knob's ridge, bore and peg circle, and the 0.8 mm gap between the magnet faces and the peg tips.
