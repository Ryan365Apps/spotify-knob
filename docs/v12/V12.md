## What v12 changes against v11, and why

1. **The deck is gone; everything sits on the plate** (Ryan, 6 Sep: "this lead to reduced z by removing the deck … the adapter plate is the only thing not on the same plane"). Audio and motion boards on 1.0 washers with M2.5 × 4 into tapped blind holes 3.0 deep in the plate's 4 mm web (16 such holes: they stop 1.0 above the duct, so the duct needs no islands under them — only the six closing screws from below keep their Ø7 islands); the converter on its gap pad; the speaker cradle now screwed from **above** too (v11: M3 from below, whose islands would have blocked fin channels at its new place); the servo lying on the plate inside a floorless frame with two ears. Thermal plan rule 15 (boards bolted to the plate) is now met by every board that has heat to lose.
2. **The adapter is three parts and the driver board stands on the Pi.** Its datasheet drawing (fetched this build; `DM-ADTTR-014` section 1.5.1) gives the outline 65 × 64, four Ø2.8 holes 3.5 from the HDMI edge and both sides and 11.5 from the flex-connector edge — the **58 × 49 Raspberry Pi pattern** — the HDMI socket 15.7 wide, 10.5 deep, **7.3 tall** (we had 6.5 assumed), the 60-pin flex connector 35 wide, 1.2 inside the opposite edge, two buttons on the left edge. The kit is "one driver board, one FPC cable, one display connect board": the panel's 46 mm flex plugs into the connect board, a 150 × 30.6 × 0.34 flat cable runs from there to the driver board. **v10 and v11 had the panel flex reaching the driver board directly — it cannot (the flex is 46 mm; the driver board's connector would have been 55 mm away).** So: driver board on four M2.5 × 12 standoffs on the Pi's holes, HDMI edge over the Pi's HDMI edge (a short ribbon loop between the two sockets, which face the same way), flex-connector edge toward 90° with an 8 mm overhang over the servo's frame (0.28 mm above it); its underside 1.2 above the heatsink's fins with 1.0 of underside parts assumed. The connect board (30 × 46 ASSUMED from its photograph) lies on a printed **bracket** bridging the port slot, its outer end resting on the port face's rail, with its panel-flex connector 8 mm from the flex's drop line; the flat cable runs inward at the bracket's level, up beside the USB-A shells, over them at 19.5, folds 45° toward 90° over the servo, Z-folds back toward 270° one cable width across, and drops onto the driver board's connector — about 95 mm of the 150 used. **The right way up, not flipped**: flipping was costed (the socket would hang into the space over the Pi's SD end, the cable would have to enter a downward-facing connector) and the seat is not set by the adapter — see 4.
3. **The Pi is carried by the closing plate.** With the deck's legs gone, four 9.5 mm square lands of the 1 mm closing plate reach into the Pi window at the Pi's holes; M2.5 × 6 countersunk screws from below go through the land and a 0.5 washer into the standoffs' bottom threads; the adapter screws into their top threads. The Pi moved to (−52, −38) — 9.5 from the ports and 10 toward 270° (Ryan's picture had it ~35 mm over, which puts its corner outside the wall). Its PC data lead is now an **up-angled USB-C plug** (ASSUMED 12 × 12.5 × 6.5 head, lead straight up): the straight plug of v11 reached r 75 through the ring at the new place, and a right-angled one collides with the HDMI plug either way. The ribbon uses **HDMI1** (HDMI0 sits behind the USB-C plug's head).
4. **The seat drops 2.4, set by the bell band, not the boards.** Ryan asked whether the 3.6 mm under the code strip on the knob's inner face could go. That 3.6 (14.9 → 18.5) is the bell band's contact zone on the bore — the skirt's bottom is at 14.9 over the halo's lip, and v11's band ran to 17.8. The band is narrowed to end 2.0 above the skirt's bottom (9.0–16.9; the contact is a line, 2 mm of overlap covers the knob's axial play), the code band moves down to 17.4–21.6, the encoder tower to 24.0, the wheels' groove to 21.9–25.1 (0.3 above the band) and the seat to 26.0–28.0. The tallest thing under the seat is the adapter's HDMI socket at 23.68, which would have allowed a 26.0 seat: **the wheels set the seat now, so flipping the adapter buys nothing.** The knob's skirt is 2.4 shorter: three knurl rows instead of four at the same 33° helix and row height. The vent slits move above the code band (21.9–25.0).
5. **The layout, in the model's frame.** Ryan's azimuths are compass bearings on his pictures (0° at the top of the picture, clockwise, ports at the bottom); the model's 0° is at the ports, anticlockwise: his 35° is the model's 145°, his 140° the model's 40°, the old speaker's "240°" was 290°. Speaker at (−36, 47), az 127.5 — boxed in on four sides (1.0 beyond the adapter's edge, 3.1 off the bell and its band, its cradle ring 1.5 inside the wall, 6 off a pillar): the pillar that was at 120° moved to 160°, and the cradle's fingers and ears are now placed by azimuth from the speaker's centre (fingers 92/212/332, ears 200/290 — away from the carriage, the wall and the adapter). Motion at az 50 r 52 (his 42 collided with the Pi's USB-A shells and the connect bracket's ear). Audio at (19, −52) — it could not be "central next to the speaker" as he drew it because that spot is the Pi's board at the new position; beside the Pi's USB-A end its USB plug is 9 mm away and the speaker leads cross the cavity. Converter at az 180, tangential, r 53–78, its inner edge 1.0 clear of the adapter's edge (its top is 1.7 higher than the adapter's underside), its outer 6 mm on the ring's flat top; the pillar the deck needed at 180 is gone. Only two of v11's ten ribs survive (az 158 and 212): the plate top is nearly full.
6. **The plate's duct is two circuits now.** The Pi's USB-C plug notch reaches r 70.6 at az 231 and, with its 2.5 mm wall, severs the front collector, so the −y side (7 channels, 6 intake grooves at 242–298°, the 308–338 plenum, the three exhaust grooves at 319–333) is its own intake-to-exhaust circuit of 9.3 cm³ and the +y side (12 channels, 14 intake grooves, the 22–52 plenum, the exhaust at 27–41) is 15.4 cm³. The checker now requires every circuit to have both an intake and an exhaust. The intake groove at 230.6 is dropped (it would leak into the notch); two channel scraps between the USB-C and HDMI notch walls are filled. The blower envelope moved into the 308–338 plenum (az 328, r 63) — where the air leaves, which is where a blower belongs. Intake 21 grooves 252 mm², exhaust 72 mm². Ribs: two (the plate top is full). Closing screws: three in the front crescent beyond the Pi window, two in the rear wedge inside the slot's walls, one in the 22–52 plenum. Ground bond an M3 blind hole at (40, 12).
7. **Mass**: core 139 g + steel ring 334 g + closing plate 25 g = 498 g; knob 94 g PETG (201 g aluminium); made parts 676 g.

Carried unchanged from v11: the Ø175.4 knob with its 20 mm rim and the derived radii; the heatsink plate's construction (web, duct, closing plate, rim ring, undercut intake, downward rear exhaust); the display bonded to the seat; the halo strip on the wall; the port face with its rail and the turned-over jack; the print review's features; the engine-turned edge.

## Bench items that decide whether v12 survives

1. **The Pi 5 without its fan, now with a board 1.2 mm over its fins**: log the SoC temperature for an hour of the real workload. The adapter blankets the heatsink; the fins' air leaves sideways under it. If it throttles: 15 mm standoffs (the seat allows the socket 2.3 more), or option B (Pi inverted on a plate boss).
2. **The adapter kit's connect board**: measure it (the model has 30 × 46 × 1.2 from a photograph, corner holes assumed) and confirm the panel's 45-way flex mates with its CN2. The bracket's bosses move to its real holes.
3. **The Pi's USB-C lead**: an up-angled plug no taller than 6.5 with the lead leaving its far end.
4. **The bell band** narrowed to 9.0–16.9: 2.0 mm of bore contact above the skirt's bottom — check the knob's axial play on the wheels stays inside it.
5. The lens, the motor base and bell, the diffuser and strip, the halo current, the Ø175 knob's inertia — as v11's items 3–7.
6. **The thermal plan's measurements** with the two-circuit duct: the passive baseline and the ducted case with a blower taped into the 308–338 plenum's envelope.
