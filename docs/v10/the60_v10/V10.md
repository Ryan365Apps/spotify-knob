# the 60 — version 10

Built 5 September 2026 from `docs/v10/V10-SPECIFICATION.md` with every default in `V10-QUESTIONS.md` approved and one correction (glue is allowed for securing the display). **Every bought part is placed as its own CAD body — the DisplayModule panel and the Raspberry Pi 5 from their vendor STEP files, the rest from `design/cad/bought-parts/` — and every part without a body is a named `_ENVELOPE`.** 0 check failures across 227 bodies in both clutch states; 11 A3 sheets; 10 printed parts + the steel plate. The assembly STEP is a tree of **24 nodes — 7 parts and 17 sub-assemblies** (one visibility toggle each; the Pi is one, each wheel is one, the port module is one) with leads, pins and shields fused into their parent body.

## What changed against the specification while building, and why

1. **The display bonds straight onto the structure's seat** (Ryan, 5 Sep: glue allowed). The spec's screwed carrier ring had nowhere to put threads: under the glass the ring is 1.5 mm thick, and outside the glass the wall is 0.2 mm away. Now: a 0.5 mm double-sided foam tape on the seat (r 62.5–65.6), three Ø2 locating nubs on the seat at 60/210/330°, no ring, no fingers. Cost removed from the stack: 1.4 mm. The display module is still one bought sub-assembly (lens bonded to the panel by its supplier) and comes off the seat by peeling the tape.
2. **The Active Cooler's fan is off; its heatsink stays.** In CAD the fan's 34 × 34 intake hole in the deck and the 65 × 64 adapter cannot both exist: the fan sits 15 mm from the Pi's centre and the adapter must have its centre within 24 mm of the axis to fit the deck, so every legal adapter position covers the hole (worked for both Pi orientations). The choices were: delete the fan; delete the cooler and invert the Pi onto a plate boss (spec option B); or drive the panel by DSI without the adapter (question 3). The fan is three screws and the heatsink stays clipped on, so that is what v10 does; the deck sits 6.9 mm above the fins with both long sides open. **This is the decision Ryan should look at first** — the alternative (option B) is silent and better thermally but needs a machined boss. The perimeter vents are 89 vertical slits (312 mm²), passive; the through-flow baffle of question 10 has no fan to serve and is not built.
3. **The servo sits on the deck and pushes a 28 mm tall carriage tab**, not tangentially through a rocker. With the Pi filling the floor's centre, no floor position exists for the servo in any azimuth; on the deck it lies at r 8–30 over the Pi, its pushrod meeting a 3 × 10 mm tab that rises from the carriage through a 6 × 10.6 slot in the deck. Same 2.4 mm lift, same 2.4 N.
4. **Pi orientation**: USB-A/Ethernet end toward the rear (the port slot starts at r 55, 9 mm clear of the stacks), USB-C/HDMI edge toward 270° beside the speaker, header edge toward the motor. The other orientations put the HDMI and USB-C plugs inside the steel plate or under the motor's carriage. The 2 × 5 header housing for 5 V + UART sits under the deck; the audio board's USB uses a right-angle plug in the upper rear USB-A socket (9 mm protrusion, ASSUMED).
5. **Deck layout** (all positions proven clash-free by the checker, all board outlines ASSUMED except the adapter's): adapter (4, −26); motion board **30 × 30** (the spec proposed 50 × 40, then 36 × 30 — the free sector is 30 × 30) at az 55; audio 25 × 40 at az 110; converter 38 × 25 × **8** (the spec said 10; 8 is what fits under the seat flange) at az 175, r ≤ 61.5; **touch controller stacked on the adapter** on 2 mm standoffs over its outer region (no deck area left). Boards sit 1.0 above the deck on captive M2.5 nuts (change 12); the adapter's HDMI socket (6.5 tall) reaches z 30.4 under the bare glass at 30.9.
6. **The halo is a bought LED strip stuck to the wall, not 90 discrete side-firing LEDs laid flat on the plate** (Ryan, 5 Sep evening). The strip (ASSUMED 5 mm wide, 1.6 thick, ~100 LEDs per metre → 46 LEDs) stands vertically on a 1.1 mm band on the structure's wall at r 72.1–73.2, its face at r 74.9 firing outward into a diffuser r 75.1–77. The band puts the strip 0.2 outside the motor's bell + band (r 73.1); over the motor's relief sector (74–106°) the strip bridges a 40 mm gap with no backing — a strip is stiff enough as a hoop. Cost: the halo band is 5.5 tall instead of 2.6, the knob's skirt starts at z 14.9 instead of 12.0 (knob 62 % of the side), and the bore meets the motor's band over only 2.9 mm of its 8.8 (grip is preload × friction, not width — watch wear on the bench). A wider strip (8 or 10 mm) raises the band by the difference and costs the knob the same.
7. **Azimuths re-derived**: wheel posts 30/150/270 (the speaker, the boards and the port slot), pillars 50/120/180/240/320 (7.5° off the port grid so the ports clear the pillars' webs), encoder 310, LRA 258. The wall gets three 15 × 5.4 windows so the wheels reach through it into the groove (the wall now runs up to the seat, unlike v9's ledge). Vents: 89 vertical slits 1.0 wide × 3.5 tall on a 2.5° grid (Ryan's review, 5 Sep: slits, not round holes — trivially printable, and many of them), skipped only at the motor's relief, the pillars' and posts' webs, the encoder tower and the LRA pad: 312 mm², double the round holes. The wheel windows and the encoder window are **open to the top** through the seat flange's outer edge so the structure prints without bridges (review items 2 and 3).
8. **Rear sockets**: the barrel jack on a **vertical** board so its axis can sit at z 3.5 (plug body Ø8.5 from −0.75 to 7.75 through a pocket in the pad); USB-C axis 4.5; jack axis 4.0; the port face's ears moved inside the wall at t ±16.
9. **Three deck legs, not four**: the Pi's USB-A-corner hole is under the speaker. The Pi hangs from the other three (M2.5 × 6 from below).
10. **The base has its detail** (review item 4, then Ryan's second pass: "more like the engine-turned bezel, chamfer top and bottom"): the plate is now **Ø154, flush with the knob**, and its edge is engine-turned — twelve smooth blocks 8° wide (10.7 mm) at 0 / 30 / 60 ... °, and between each pair a field of twelve square-cut vertical grooves 1.2 wide × 1.0 deep on a 2.4 mm pitch (144 grooves; ~1.0 mm land at each end of a field), all cut in plan with the outline so the laser or waterjet makes the pattern for free — with a **1.0 mm chamfer on both the top and the bottom outer edge** (a lathe or CNC pass after cutting; the grooves run out through the top chamfer, as on a machined bezel). The top chamfer runs r 76.0–77.0, so the diffuser's seat (r 75.1–76.0) is still on solid steel (checked). The block at 0° is lost to the port slot. The diffuser leans out from r 76.0 at the bottom to 77.0 at the top, so its bottom edge is inboard of the chamfer's start and the profile reads plate → chamfer in → diffuser leaning back out → knob, one silhouette at Ø154 with a V-shaped shadow line at the plate top; the pad is Ø148 at its top face, 0.5 smaller again at the desk.
11. **The assembly STEP is a tree, not a flat list** (Ryan, 5 Sep evening: too many toggles). Chips' leads, bearing rings and shields, socket pins and the Pi's connectors are fused into their parent body; the bodies are grouped into 17 sub-assemblies (pi5, display_module, motor_carriage_unit, wheel_30/150/270, servo_unit, speaker_unit, encoder_unit, lra, adapter_unit, audio_unit, motion_unit, port_module, plugs_external, cables_internal, halo) beside 7 top-level parts (plate, structure, knob, deck, tape, converter, pad). The checker still tests every solid individually.
12. **Print review** (Ryan, 5 Sep: "focus on the internal structure and the ability to print this part"; "does it even need that area above the wheel?"). No — the seat flange above each wheel did nothing but hang over the window, so it is **cut away entirely** (a 15.2 mm notch through the flange at 30 / 150 / 270; the glass bridges it, the tape ring is unbroken). The rest of the review, from `src/printcheck.py`, which reads every printed part in its print orientation and lists each downward face that is not on the bed, each bridge with its span, and each sideways hole: the **motor's window in the wall** is opened up to the seat (it had a 27 mm roof to bridge); the **encoder tower** is 25.1 tall with a 2 mm roof over the sensor pocket (it had a 0.3 mm one) and its board slot runs open through the seat, with a 0.25 crush rib so the 8 × 11 board is a friction fit dropped in before the display goes on; the three blind plate-screw insert holes get **45° ends**; the **deck's 0.8 mm insert bosses on its top face are gone** — printed top face down they left the whole face hanging 0.8 above the bed — replaced by fourteen hex pockets in the top face holding **M2.5 nuts, the boards on the nuts (1.0 proud), M2.5 × 4 screws from above** (inserts under the deck were tried and fail: only 1.28 mm exists between the deck and the motor bell, 0.5 to the Pi's USB stack); the servo tray gets the two ears its screws never had; the **port face prints lying on its outer face** so its three socket holes are vertical; the **speaker cradle's fingers stand from the plate**, not from the ring's top; the **knurl runs out through the knob's bottom chamfer** so its foot is not 68 downward ledges when the knob prints top face down. What remains needing support is exactly two rings on the structure, both hidden faces: the seat flange's underside (r 62–70.5, 8.5 wide, 20.4 above the bed) and the diffuser lip's underside (2.8 wide, 5.5 above the bed). A 45° cone under the flange was tried and rejected: it collides with the HDMI plug on the adapter (top z 27.0 at r 62–64) and the flex route, and would still leave a 3.4 mm ledge.

## Bench items that decide whether v10 survives

1. **The Pi 5 without its fan at ~4 W in the closed cavity**: log the SoC temperature for an hour of the real workload. If it throttles, the answer is option B (Pi inverted on a plate boss), not a bigger hole.
2. **The adapter's panel connector** must mate with the 45-way 0.3 mm flex (question 17) and its HDMI socket must be on the edge the model assumes (its −x edge as placed, facing the ribbon).
3. **The lens**: Ø140 × 2.5 with the touch tail at the flex edge — anything else changes the seat, the nubs and the lip overlap.
4. **Motor base and bell** as v9 (base ≤ Ø31, bell from ≥ 4.3 above its base).
5. **The 1.9 mm diffuser** 0.2 in front of the strip — opal grade; and the strip itself (width, LED pitch, adhesive) — the model assumes 5 × 1.6.
6. **Halo current**: 3.2 A at full white; the converter module must be a real 10 A part.

## Printing (`src/printcheck.py`: every part in its print orientation — downward faces, bridges, sideways holes)

Rules applied, in the usual FDM (fused-filament) terms: a face is self-supporting when it is steeper than 45° from horizontal; a flat downward face prints as a bridge when both ends rest on material and the span is short (everything here is under 13 mm); a hole with a sideways axis sags at its top, so holes are printed vertical; walls are two perimeters or more (nothing here is under 1.0 mm — the crush rib is 0.6 and is meant to squash); blind holes end in a 45° point; the face that has to be flat and smooth goes on the bed; chamfers, not fillets, on bed edges (the plate is steel, the knob's bed edge is the 2.5 × 45° chamfer). Sources: [Prusa, Modeling with 3D printing in mind](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135) (45–60° overhangs; 0.45 / 0.9 / 1.35 mm for 1 / 2 / 3 perimeters; 0.3 mm clearance for moving fits; bed faces are the smooth ones; chamfer rather than fillet on the bed); [Rahix, Design for 3D-Printing](https://blog.rahix.de/design-for-3d-printing/) (teardrop or vertical holes; ±0.1 mm per surface; clearance ≥ 2 × the printer's tolerance); [UltiMaker, Design for FFF](https://ultimaker.com/learn/design-for-fff-3d-printing-maximize-your-success/).
