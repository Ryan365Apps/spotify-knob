# Section C — Automotive rotary controllers as feel references

Research strand for the 60, by Cadrane (the Ø135 mm desk dial with sixty magnetic detents and a motor layer on top). Every figure below is cited or marked unverified. Compiled 2026-09-02.

---

## C1. BMW iDrive — the rotary infotainment controller

### Who makes it and how it works inside

- **First generation (E65 7 Series, 2001): a true force-feedback knob.** The knob itself was manufactured by ALPS Electric, and housed "an encoder and belt drive" — a belt wrapping a drive spindle mounted directly on a motor shaft — with haptic profiles designed by Immersion Corp. (work started 1998). Immersion's profiles contained instructions for "detents, hard stops, 'hills,' and other touch-sensitive responses", changed screen-by-screen: speaker balance got discrete detents, while "hills" acted as permeable barriers between screen regions. Immersion's framing is the important part: "The controller doesn't know what it's controlling but knows what 'feel' is appropriate" per screen. Source: [Electronic Design, "Can BMW's iDrive Pass Its Road Test Now?"](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/boards/article/21760094/can-bmws-idrive-pass-its-road-test-now).
- **The force feedback was then deleted.** Wikipedia's BMW iDrive article records that the March 2007 CCC (Car Communication Computer, the second-generation head unit) software/hardware update "removes the haptic feedback from the iDrive controller" — BMW retreated from motorised feel to plain mechanical detents. Owners on BMW forums note the motorised versions had "hard spots to tell you when you've reached the end of the menu" (variable end stops). Sources: [Wikipedia, BMW iDrive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_iDrive); [BimmerFest thread "iDrive force feedback and end of menus"](https://www.bimmerfest.com/threads/idrive-force-feedback-and-end-of-menus.1364259/) (forum, snippet only — thread is behind a fetch-wall).
- **Later generations: Preh GmbH.** From the F01 7 Series (2008) onward the iDrive controller is a Preh part; Preh's own releases confirm it supplies "the latest iDrive controller generation" for the 7, 6 and 5 Series, 5 Series GT and X3, and describes itself as one of "only three or four companies globally" that mass-produce central control systems at this level. The 2008 unit "responds to turning, tilting and pressing" with "precise haptic" and a "backlit corona ring" for night visibility. Preh continued through the 2025 5 Series / i5 controllers. Sources: [Preh press release, Feb 2011](https://www.preh.com/en/press/press-archives/detail/preh-supplies-idrive-controller-for-the-new-x3); [Electronic Design on the Preh 7-Series controller, Dec 2008](https://www.electronicdesign.com/markets/automotive/article/21797412/preh-supplies-idrive-controller-for-bmw-7series); [Automotive News, "Preh makes controllers for BMW"](https://www.autonews.com/article/20100524/OEM01/100529942/preh-makes-controllers-for-bmw/); [GM Insights market report naming Preh for the 2025 5 Series](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/automotive-haptic-feedback-rotary-controllers-market).
- **Detent count:** an Alibaba-hosted maintenance guide claims a "24-pulse-per-revolution" encoder and 24 detents matching teeth on an internal wheel ([source](https://carinterior.alibaba.com/tips/f20-rotary-knob-maintenance-guide)). That page reads like machine-generated content and its "±0.03 N·m" detent-torque figure is uncited; treat **24 detents/rev as unverified**. No BMW or Preh document stating the count was found in this pass. Teardown threads confirm the mechanism: a sensor on the PCB "registers little teeth on the rotating mechanism" and a thin plastic guide ring inside the knob is the common wear failure that makes rotation feel "gritty" ([BIMMERPOST disassembly thread](https://bmwi.bimmerpost.com/forums/showthread.php?p=26587017); [X3 forum disassembly thread](https://x3.xbimmers.com/forums/showthread/2006148/anyone-know-how-to-dissassemble-idrive-controller); [Go-Parts failure guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-control-switch-bmw-530e-bmw-540i-bmw-m5-2017-2023)). So the production iDrive controller is spring-follower-on-toothed-ring (spring-and-cam family), not magnetic and — after 2007 — not motorised.

### Surface and light

- **Ceramic option (F01 7 Series, "Ceramic Surround"):** Preh's optional ceramics design "strengthens the distinctive haptic feel" and gives a "cool touch" surface — the same argument the 60 makes for mass and thermal feel in metal. Source: [Electronic Design, Dec 2008](https://www.electronicdesign.com/markets/automotive/article/21797412/preh-supplies-idrive-controller-for-bmw-7series).
- **Glass/crystal option ("CraftedClarity", G05 X5 onward):** hand-polished glass made with Swarovski, covering the gear selector (with illuminated "X"), the iDrive controller and the start/stop button. The controller's arrow markings "are illuminated from below" and the set ties into the ambient-light colour; a US-market listing prices the option around $900 CAD on a 2024 X5 (dealer/forum figures, treat as indicative). Sources: [Competition BMW explainer](https://www.competitionbmw.com/2019-bmw-x5-glass-control/); [carolinasbmwmoa.org "What is BMW CraftedClarity?"](https://carolinasbmwmoa.org/what-is-bmw-crafted-clarity/); [IND Distribution OEM glass controller listing (product photos)](https://ind-distribution.com/products/bmw-oem-real-glass-idrive-controller); [G05 owners' thread on glass controls](https://g05.bimmerpost.com/forums/showthread/2208483/glass-controls). Aftermarket-crystal buyers report the genuine article's virtues by contrast: the crystal knob "has a weightier feel over the stock version" but clone units need a more forceful press and click audibly where OEM buttons feel like "an iPhone home button" — mechanical click noise reads as cheap ([G05 glass controls thread, p.5](https://g05.bimmerpost.com/forums/showthread/1587224/glass-controls/page/5); [Amazon crystal knob listing/reviews](https://www.amazon.com/Crystal-Multimedia-Controller-Knob-Upgrade/dp/B081PM1QFS)).
- Since 2012 (NBT, the "Next Big Thing" head unit) the knob top is a capacitive touchpad for handwriting input — the top surface became smooth glass-like plastic, the grip surface stayed on the rim. Source: [Wikipedia, BMW iDrive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_iDrive).

### The retreat, and what BMW says was lost

- BMW dropped the controller entirely: iDrive 9 (2023) went touchscreen-only on most models, and the Neue Klasse "iDrive X" (iX3, 2025) has no controller at all. BMW's stated reasons: usage data showed the controller "declining sharply over the last few model generations", used mostly while parked; and the new Panoramic Display breaks the list-based navigation a knob needs ("for an iDrive controller, everything needed to be in a list"). Reviewers concede speed but admit reaching for the missing knob out of habit. Sources: [Wikipedia, BMW iDrive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_iDrive); [BMWBlog, "Will We Miss the iDrive Controller?"](https://www.bmwblog.com/2025/10/21/bmw-idrive-controller-end-future-idrive-x/); [BMWBlog, "BMW Explains Why The iDrive Controller Is Dead"](https://www.bmwblog.com/2025/01/11/bmw-explains-why-idrive-controller-dead/); [BMWBlog, reasons for the iX3 change](https://www.bmwblog.com/2025/09/05/bmw-ix3-idrive-controller-replaced/).

## C2. Audi MMI — the Multi Media Interface rotary dial

- The MMI (Multi Media Interface) dial was a knurled push-rotate controller on the console; later versions (MMI 3G+) added joystick nudges and a touchpad. Forum guidance on healthy units: the knob "should rotate smoothly in both directions with distinct detents (clicks)". No verified detent count found. Sources: [Audizine, "Replace MMI control Knob?"](https://www.audizine.com/threads/replace-mmi-control-knob.798831/); [Go-Parts Audi console guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/console-panel-audi-s5-audi-q7-audi-a5-2017-2025).
- Audi killed the dial in 2018–2019 (A6/A7/A8/A4 with MMI Touch Response, twin haptic touchscreens). Contemporary coverage: the screens "vibrate with haptic feedback to confirm a selection"; reviewer reaction was split — Consumer Reports-style commentary found the old "rotary dial controller was much easier to use than the new A6's haptic screens, which require more unsafe eye time away from the road", while Engadget's hands-on writer expected to miss the dial and didn't. Sources: [AudiWorld, "Say Goodbye to Audi's MMI Rotary Dial"](https://www.audiworld.com/forums/a4-b9-platform-discussion-212/say-goodbye-audis-mmi-rotary-dial-2912418/); [Engadget hands-on, 2018](https://www.engadget.com/2018-07-13-audi-mmi-touch-response-hands-on.html); [Consumer Reports A6 preview](https://www.consumerreports.org/sedans/2019-audi-a6-sedan-preview).
- Audi is now reversing course: 2026 reporting says Audi will reintroduce physical controls because buyers "appreciate controls they can find without taking their eyes off the road". Source: [Carscoops, "Audi Is Reversing Its Biggest Interior Decision Of The Decade"](https://www.carscoops.com/2026/07/audi-interior-buttons-return/).

## C3. Mercedes-Benz COMAND — the Cockpit Management and Data system controller

- The COMAND (Cockpit Management and Data) rotary controller was praised for exactly the quality the 60 is chasing: Cars.com valued it "because of its hefty, weighted feel and consistent performance" and called it "the quickest and easiest method" for the interface. Source: [Cars.com, "Five Things to Know About Mercedes' Comand System"](https://www.cars.com/articles/five-things-to-know-about-mercedes-comand-system-1420680637470/).
- Mechanically it was fragile in one specific way: the most common failure is a broken internal **plastic** shaft, fixed with an aftermarket metal shaft kit — a lesson in not putting plastic in the torque path of a handled object. Source: [Go-Parts Mercedes controller failure guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/console-panel-mercedes-benz-c-class-mercedes-benz-glc-class-mercedes-benz-cls-class-2008-2022).
- Mercedes replaced the knob with a touchpad (W213 E-Class, 2016), then MBUX (Mercedes-Benz User Experience) touchscreens. The touchpad "hasn't caught on" with driving staff despite better handwriting input; the knob's directness was the loss. By 2026 Mercedes is re-adding physical controls, acknowledging touch "went too far". Sources: [paultan.org on the W213 touchpad](https://paultan.org/2016/03/10/mercedes-benz-comand-touchpad-appears-in-e-class/); [paultan.org quick look, 2017](https://paultan.org/2017/09/13/quick-look-new-mercedes-benz-comand-touchpad/); [mensenauto on MBUX](https://www.mensenauto.com/blog/2018/mbux-part-1/); [The Weekly Driver on Mercedes and the Euro NCAP button rules](https://theweeklydriver.com/2026/08/euro-ncap-2026-physical-controls-mercedes-buttons/).

## C4. Porsche PCM — Porsche Communication Management

- Porsche kept a small rotary controller ahead of the gear selector for scrolling PCM (Porsche Communication Management, the infotainment system) menus, plus a separate mechanical volume knob on the console in the Panamera and 911 — Porsche never bet the whole interface on one hero knob. Sources: [CarExpert PCM review](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/porsche-communication-management-pcm-infotainment-review); [Porsche Newsroom, Panamera interior](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/panamera/Interior.html).
- The aftermarket fills the tactility gap: machined, ribbed/knurled replacement volume and climate knobs in satin chrome are a whole product category for Porsche interiors, sold on "precision-machined ribbed grooves [that] enhance tactile feel and grip" ([AutoTecknic replacement knob](https://autotecknic.com/products/autotecknic-replacement-climate-control-volume-knob-porsche); [Rennlist thread on replacement PCM knobs](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-958-2011-2018/1200778-replacement-radio-pcm-3-1-knobs-set-p-n.html)). No professional review of PCM detent quality specifically was found; that gap is noted rather than filled with invention.

## C5. Bentley — organ stops and knurl as brand signature

- Bentley's air-vent controls are "organ stops" — pull-push plungers named after pipe-organ stops — with diamond-knurled bullseye vent rims, latterly machined in titanium on the Batur. They are the industry's clearest case of a control that exists to be *fondled*: dealer and press material leans on the words "knurled", "machined" and "hand-polished", and dealers post close-up "satisfying" videos of just operating them. Sources: [Bentley Motors on diamond knurling on the bullseye vent](https://www.facebook.com/BentleyMotors/photos/diamond-knurling-on-the-bullseye-vent-in-the-new-continental-gt-convertible-give/2679677958732149/); [H.R. Owen video of the knurled organ-stop vent controls](https://www.facebook.com/HROwenSpecialistCars/videos/satisfying-exquisitely-crafted-bentley-gt-knurled-organ-stop-air-vent-controls-s/2810634839190055/); [Watch I Love on the Batur's machined-titanium organ stops and bullseyes](https://watchilove.com/2025/04/bentley-batur-convertible-makes-public-debut/); [Spear's Continental GT test drive](https://spearswms.com/test-drive-bentleys-pretty-perfect-new-continental-gt/); [genuine organ-stop setting knob as a spare part](https://www.flyingspares.com/setting-knob-3w0819790b.html) (image on the listing).
- The organ stop is linear, not rotary, but the lesson transfers: no screen, no light, purely mechanical travel with a firm end — and it survived every Bentley interior redesign because owners notice it. The knurl is functional (grip on a smooth plated cylinder) before it is decorative.

## C6. Rolls-Royce — the Spirit of Ecstasy rotary controller

- Rolls-Royce (BMW Group) ships the iDrive controller rebadged as the "Spirit of Ecstasy Rotary Controller" on Ghost and Phantom — same electronics, different jewellery, sometimes with an engraved glass top. One Ghost review surfaced in search says the controller "comes chunkier than in a BMW and with a slightly smoother feel" (**exact outlet unverified** — the line appears in search-result aggregation across the What Car? and HotCars Ghost reviews and could not be confirmed against the paywalled page). ## C7. Volvo — the Orrefors crystal gear selector ("Crystal Eye")

- Volvo's shift-by-wire lever on top trims is solid Orrefors crystal, hand-made in Sweden with techniques Autocar describes as near-identical to 18th-century glasswork; the mechanism underneath was developed with Kongsberg Automotive. Owners describe a "sturdy feel and soft response"; detractors call it "a bit ridiculous". It does nothing functional — Autocar-adjacent coverage is explicit that it changes no shift behaviour — it exists to give the hand something cold, dense and optically deep. Sources: [Autocar, "How Volvo makes its crystal glass gearsticks"](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/features/how-volvo-makes-its-crystal-glass-gearsticks) (paywalled on fetch; details from search rendering); [Kongsberg Automotive on the crystal shifter](https://www.kongsbergautomotive.com/innovation/an-inside-look/crystal-shifter/); [Custom of Sweden parts listing with photos](https://www.customofsweden.se/en/bildelar/volvo/volvo-orrefors-vaxelvaljare); [dealer explainer](https://www.osteenvolvocarsofjacksonville.com/blog/2024/february/28/shifting-into-elegance-orrefors-crystal-masterpiece-for-volvo-cars.htm).
- Relevance to the 60: crystal/glass as a *cap* on a mechanical control reads as luxury only when the mechanism under it is beyond reproach; the Volvo lever pivots in a conventional gate, so all the crystal has to do is be heavy and clear.

## C8. Genesis — the crystal-sphere shift-by-wire controller (GV60)

- The GV60's "Crystal Sphere" is the most theatrical rotary control in production: with the car off, the console shows an illuminated crystal-glass sphere with a laser-engraved internal "G-Matrix" pattern; on power-up the whole assembly rotates 180° about its horizontal axis to present a knurled, metal-finished shift-by-wire dial. The flip mechanism uses a shaft position sensor, motor control for alignment, and a worm gear/worm wheel that prevents back-driving; it can be rotated manually as an emergency fallback, refuses to flip while charging, and was durability-tested against spilled cola, ketchup, yogurt and honey. The sphere glows within the ambient-light system and shifts red in reverse. Source: [CarBuzz, "The Engineering Behind The World's Only Disappearing Crystal Sphere Gear Lever"](https://carbuzz.com/genesis-gv60-boasts-the-worlds-only-disappearing-crystal-sphere-gear-lever/); [Genesis USA how-to video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WRNyg4z2vNA).
- Design intent, from Genesis chief creative officer Luc Donckerwolke: the sphere exists "because the GV60 is so quiet that it needed a special device to prompt the driver" — a state indicator you read by shape, not by screen. Source: [CarBuzz, same article](https://carbuzz.com/genesis-gv60-boasts-the-worlds-only-disappearing-crystal-sphere-gear-lever/).
- Other Genesis models (GV80 etc.) use a knurled rotary shifter plus a second, similar-sized rotary for infotainment; one reviewer flags the obvious failure mode — "providing an adjacent control similar in size but not function creates a recipe for confusion". Source: [The Auto Channel 2025 GV80 review](https://www.theautochannel.com/news/2024/10/02/1437779-2025-genesis-gv80-3-5t-prestige-awd-review-by-david.html).

## C9. Detent mechanisms across the field — what is actually inside

- **Spring-and-cam (toothed ring + spring follower)** is the production norm: every non-motorised iDrive, MMI (Multi Media Interface) and COMAND (Cockpit Management and Data) controller torn down or repaired in forum guides works this way, and its known failure modes are worn plastic followers/guide rings (gritty feel) and broken plastic shafts. Sources as in C1 and C3; general mechanism explainer: [Firgelli, "Detent Mechanism Explained"](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/detent).
- **Motorised force feedback** was tried at the very start (E65 iDrive, Alps motor + belt + Immersion profiles: programmable detents, hard stops, "hills") and abandoned in 2007, then partially revived: 2024–2025 BMW controllers are described as having "high-resolution tactile motors" with "programmable feedback that changes based on the drive mode and infotainment context" and "haptic detents [that] provide click points" (market-report language — treat capability claims as vendor-grade until a teardown confirms). Sources: [Electronic Design Immersion article](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/boards/article/21760094/can-bmws-idrive-pass-its-road-test-now); [Wikipedia, BMW iDrive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_iDrive); [GM Insights haptic rotary controllers market report](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/automotive-haptic-feedback-rotary-controllers-market).
- **Magnetic and magnetorheological detents** are the patent frontier rather than the automotive production norm: US patent 10024690 covers an "incremental rotary encoder using hall effect sensors and magnetic detents"; a cluster of patents (US10528137, US10310603, US10466788, US12447823, US12473952) covers magnetorheological-fluid haptic knobs for vehicles. The relevant academic root is Immersion-era work: ["Rotary haptic knob for vehicular instrument controls"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3945755_Rotary_haptic_knob_for_vehicular_instrument_controls) (Haptics Symposium 2002) — force-feedback knobs that "emulate the feel of various conventional control knobs through mechanical springs, detents, and hard stops". Patent sources: [US10024690](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10024690); [US10528137](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10528137); [US12447823](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12447823); [US6154201, Immersion control knob with force feedback](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6154201).
- **Capacitive fake knobs** exist too — Panasonic's "Magic Knob" is a rotary ring for touchscreens with 32 mechanical detents (20 on the small-diameter variant) and a rotational torque spec of 25 mN·m ±30% (the datasheet page writes "25 mN/m", evidently a typo for mN·m) — useful as the only public, hard torque number found for an automotive-grade detent ring in this pass. Source: [Panasonic Industry, Magic Knob](https://industry.panasonic.eu/products/components/rotary-input-devices/magic-knob).
- **The 60's architecture — permanent-magnet detents plus a motor layer — is therefore not a copy of any automotive production controller.** Cars did spring-cam (cheap, fixed feel), or motor-only (E65: dead with power off, and BMW retreated from it). The 60's split (magnets give an unpowered fixed scale, motor adds dynamics) matches the direction of the magnetorheological/magnetic patent activity more than any shipping car part.

## C10. The retreat from rotary controllers, and what reviewers say was lost

- The industry removed knobs for cost and "clean" screens; it is now legislated back. From January 2026, Euro NCAP (the European New Car Assessment Programme) requires physical controls for five functions (turn signals, hazards, horn, wipers, eCall emergency call) to reach a five-star rating, and scores intuitive physical controls beyond that. Sources: [ETSC](https://etsc.eu/cars-will-need-buttons-not-just-touchscreens-to-get-a-5-star-euro-ncap-safety-rating/); [carwow](https://www.carwow.co.uk/news/9964/euro-ncap-2026-safety-rules); [The Weekly Driver on the expanded nine-control interpretation](https://theweeklydriver.com/2026/08/euro-ncap-2026-physical-controls-mercedes-buttons/).
- What reviewers say was lost, consistently: **eyes-free operation** ("tactile feedback means you don't need to take your eyes off the road"), speed (touchscreen tasks measurably slower), and reliability of input under vibration. Mercedes, VW, Hyundai, Audi are all restoring physical controls; IEEE Spectrum headlines it flatly: "Touchscreens Are Out, and Tactile Controls Are Back". Sources: [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/touchscreens); [Design News](https://www.designnews.com/automotive-engineering/automakers-shift-gears-physical-controls-make-a-comeback-in-vehicle-design); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/joshmax/2026/03/30/knobs-returning-to-some-automakers-vehicles-to-the-relief-of-many/); [Autoblog on manufacturers bringing buttons back](https://www.autoblog.com/news/which-car-manufacturers-are-bringing-back-physical-buttons).
- The nuance from BMW's side: the controller lost not because knobs are bad but because *in a car* the screen moved out of the knob's reach-and-list paradigm, and usage collapsed. On a desk, where the hand rests beside the object all day and the task (volume/seek) is one-dimensional, none of BMW's stated reasons apply. Source: [BMWBlog, "BMW Explains Why The iDrive Controller Is Dead"](https://www.bmwblog.com/2025/01/11/bmw-explains-why-idrive-controller-dead/).

## Recommendations for the 60

1. **Keep the magnet-plus-motor split, and treat the E65 iDrive as the cautionary tale for the motor layer.** BMW shipped motor-only haptics in 2001 and deleted them in the March 2007 update ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_iDrive)); a knob whose feel exists only under power ages badly and fails to nothing. The 60's sixty permanent-magnet detents already avoid this — the motor should only ever *add* (end stops, "hills" between zones in the Immersion sense, per-context damping), never be required for the baseline click. Adopt Immersion's screen-agnostic architecture explicitly: the knob firmware knows feel profiles, not application state ([Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/boards/article/21760094/can-bmws-idrive-pass-its-road-test-now)).
2. **Put no plastic in the torque path, and no sliding plastic in the detent path.** The documented field failures of COMAND (broken internal plastic shaft — [Go-Parts](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/console-panel-mercedes-benz-c-class-mercedes-benz-glc-class-mercedes-benz-cls-class-2008-2022)) and iDrive (worn plastic guide ring turning rotation "gritty" — [Go-Parts](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-control-switch-bmw-530e-bmw-540i-bmw-m5-2017-2023)) are both plastic wear parts inside metal-skinned controllers, and both destroy exactly the quality the metal promised. The 60's magnetic detents (non-contact) and V-wheel running gear should be audited for any plastic element whose wear the hand would feel; for scale, the only published automotive detent-torque figure found is Panasonic's Magic Knob at 25 mN·m ±30% for a light 32-detent console ring ([Panasonic](https://industry.panasonic.eu/products/components/rotary-input-devices/magic-knob)) — the 60's Ø135 rim should sit well above that, but the actual target must come from the bench rig (open questions A–F in the 60's decision index, `docs/DECISIONS.md`), not from this figure.
3. **Light from below, through openings — never a lit surface.** The consistent luxury pattern is an unlit monochrome control with light escaping from beneath or within: Preh's iDrive "backlit corona ring" ([Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/markets/automotive/article/21797412/preh-supplies-idrive-controller-for-bmw-7series)), CraftedClarity's arrows "illuminated from below" through glass facets ([carolinasbmwmoa](https://carolinasbmwmoa.org/what-is-bmw-crafted-clarity/)), the Genesis sphere glowing within ambient light and turning red only for a state that matters (reverse — [CarBuzz](https://carbuzz.com/genesis-gv60-boasts-the-worlds-only-disappearing-crystal-sphere-gear-lever/)). This matches the 60's law that colour only ever comes from light, and endorses the halo-under-the-knob placement over any illuminated marking on the knurled ring itself.
