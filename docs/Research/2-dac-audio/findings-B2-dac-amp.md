# Findings B2 — DAC chip, headphone amplification, and volume mapping

Research strand for the 60's audio output: the DAC (digital-to-analogue converter) chip fed by I2S (inter-IC sound, the standard serial audio bus) from the ESP32-P4, the headphone drive stage behind the 3.5 mm jack, and how a 60-detent physical dial maps onto volume control.

All stock and price figures were checked on 2 September 2026 and are snapshots; distributor stock moves daily. Prices are USD as published by the distributor pages fetched. "Quote required" means the figure is not published, not that it is unknown to the supplier. Anything not read from a fetched source is marked **unverified**.

## 1. The ES9219 variants situation (verified)

The project's known finding — "ES9219C is obsolete, ES9219Q is the successor" — is corroborated, with one refinement:

- The ES9219 datasheet v1.2 ordering table lists exactly two orderable parts: **ES9219Q** (40-pin QFN — quad flat no-lead package — without MQA) and **ES9219MQ** (40-pin QFN, with the MQA renderer, MQA being the licensed "Master Quality Authenticated" codec). The ES9219C does not appear in the ordering table, though the datasheet's own measurement notes say "Measurements taken with ES9219C" — same die, superseded package/SKU. Source: [ES9219 Datasheet v1.2, Table 23 Ordering Information](https://www.esstech.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/ES9219-Datasheet-v1.2.pdf) (extracted from the PDF).
- Qudelix (who ship the ES9219C in the 5K) documents the lineage: "ESS discontinued ES9218. From Nov 2021, 5K DAC/AMP is manufactured with ES9219C, which is pin-to-pin compatible with ES9218P" — [Qudelix ES9219C page](https://www.qudelix.com/blogs/5k-dac-amp/es9218p). A Qudelix forum post states the ES9219C is end-of-life and replaced by the ES9219MQ ([forum post](https://forum.qudelix.com/post/comparison-between-es9218p-and-es9219c-12549331), statement seen in a search summary — **unverified at the primary source**).
- Mouser lists a product change notification document on its ES9219C page ([Mouser ES9219C](https://www.mouser.com/ProductDetail/ESS-Technology/ES9219C)); the page fetch timed out, so the PCN (product change notification) content is **unverified**.

**Design consequence:** specify **ES9219Q** (no MQA licence baggage) for anything ES9219-based. ES9219MQ distributor availability was not checked — quote required.

## 3. Headphone amplification

### Findings

**Integrated drivers.** The CS43131 drives 30 mW per channel into 32 ohm and 5 mW into 600 ohm at -115 dB THD+N / 130 dB-A dynamic range, ground-centred (no output capacitor), from a VP supply that accepts 3.0–5.25 V — i.e. raw USB 5 V ([CS43131 datasheet DS1155F2](https://statics.cirrus.com/pubs/proDatasheet/CS43131_DS1155F2.pdf), Tables 3-2 and 3-4, extracted from the PDF). The ES9219Q drives 2.0 Vrms with -112 dB THD+N into 300 ohm and -106 dB THD+N at 300 mVrms into 32 ohm, from 1.8 V + 3.3 V rails with two internal 500 kHz charge pumps, 69 mW total draw in HiFi 2 V mode ([ES9219 datasheet v1.2](https://www.esstech.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/ES9219-Datasheet-v1.2.pdf), extracted from the PDF).

Arithmetic (not a datasheet claim): 2 Vrms into 300 ohm is 13.3 mW; 30 mW into 32 ohm is 0.98 Vrms. So an integrated driver gives roughly 1 Vrms of usable swing into 32 ohm loads and the full 2 Vrms into 300 ohm loads.

**Discrete stages.**
- **TI TPA6120A2**: 1.5 W per channel into 32 ohm at under 0.0003 % THD+N, SNR 128 dB-A — but its supply range is **plus/minus 5 V to plus/minus 15 V** ([TI datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tpa6120a2.pdf)). From a USB 5 V budget that means a boost converter plus inverter, extra board area, and converter noise to manage. Mouser stock 2,730 at $3.44 @25; DigiKey 0 in stock with a 3,000-unit minimum at $2.9368 ([OEMsTrade](https://www.oemstrade.com/search/TPA6120A2RGYR)).
- **TI OPA1622**: dual audio op-amp with +145/-130 mA output drive, -119.2 dB THD+N while delivering 100 mW into 32 ohm, supply **plus/minus 2 V to plus/minus 18 V**, 2.6 mA per channel quiescent ([TI datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/opa1622.pdf)). Rails of plus/minus 2.5 V derived from USB 5 V (charge-pump inverter) are inside its range. Mouser stock 5,999 at $5.31 @25 / $4.49 @250 ([OEMsTrade](https://www.oemstrade.com/search/OPA1622IDRCR)).
- **TI LME49600**: 250 mA audio buffer used inside an op-amp's feedback loop; currently listed active at TI after an earlier discontinuation scare ([TI page](https://www.ti.com/product/LME49600), [diyAudio discussion](https://diyaudio.com/forums/headphone-systems/295308-chip-lme49610-successor.html)). It is a buffer, not an amplifier — needs a companion op-amp and dual rails.

**Output impedance target.** The accepted guidance is NwAvGuy's **1/8th rule**: "most headphones work best when the output impedance is less than 1/8th the headphone impedance", with "under 2 ohms" as the practical blanket figure and near-zero as what high-end sources achieve ([NwAvGuy, Headphone & Amp Impedance, Feb 2011](https://nwavguy.blogspot.com/2011/02/headphone-amp-impedance.html) — fetched). For 16 ohm in-ears the 1/8th rule gives 2 ohm, hence the market converged on **under 1 ohm** for universal compatibility — e.g. Audio Science Review measured the Apple USB-C dongle at 0.9 ohm output impedance (via search summary of the [ASR forum thread](https://www.audiosciencereview.com/forum/index.php?threads/apple-dongle-usb-c-to-3-5-mm-adc-quality-with-earpods-free-headsets.34219/)).

**One jack for headphones and line-level.** Measured dongles do not switch anything: a ground-centred output at 1 ohm or less source impedance and 2 Vrms full scale drives a line input (typically 10 kohm or more) as-is — 2 Vrms is the consumer full-scale line convention, and both the ES9219 (2.0 Vrms) and CS43131 (+1 dB mode, 5.70 Vpp = 2.0 Vrms) hit it. What real designs add is a **gain / output-level switch** (the Qudelix 5K exposes 1 Vrms and 2 Vrms modes on its 3.5 mm jack, per the [ASR Qudelix 5K review thread](https://www.audiosciencereview.com/forum/index.php?threads/qudelix-5k-bluetooth-dac-headphone-amp.17386/), via search summary) so sensitive in-ears are not confined to the bottom of the volume range. The CS43131's built-in **load impedance detection** (DC and AC measurement, plug-in detect — datasheet System Features page) exists precisely to auto-select that gain.

### Assessment (recommendation-flavoured, separated from findings)

For a 32–300 ohm target under Audio Science Review-style scrutiny, an integrated-driver chip is enough, and it is what the best-measuring dongles ship. 30 mW into 32 ohm (CS43131) is about 1 Vrms — loud on any typical-sensitivity 32 ohm headphone; 2 Vrms into 300 ohm covers the Sennheiser HD 600-class load. A TPA6120A2 stage would buy power this jack's audience cannot use, at the cost of a dual-rail supply, board area the 25 x 25 mm footprint does not have, and a noise-management problem next to a motor and Wi-Fi. The only discrete stage that fits the power budget and footprint gracefully is an OPA1622 on plus/minus 2.5 V charge-pump rails behind a line-out DAC (CS43198 or AK4493S) — a two-chip solution that must beat a one-chip solution already rated at 130 dB-A dynamic range; the measured-product evidence in section 5 says it generally does not. Target **under 1 ohm output impedance** (the integrated drivers deliver this natively) and **2 Vrms maximum output** with a high/low gain switch driven by impedance detection or a settings toggle.

## 4. Volume control and the 60-detent dial

### Findings: digital versus analogue volume

- **Digital volume** attenuates the signal before the DAC's modulator. Its cost: every 6 dB of digital attenuation discards one bit of the source, but with a 32-bit processing path and a converter noise floor around 130 dB-A, attenuation disappears into analogue noise long before it audibly truncates 16- or 24-bit content. The CS43131 implements 0 to -127.5 dB in 0.5 dB steps with soft ramp ([datasheet DS1155F2](https://statics.cirrus.com/pubs/proDatasheet/CS43131_DS1155F2.pdf), volume register section, extracted); the ES9219 implements 0 to -127 dB in 0.5 dB steps, each step transitioned through up to 64 intermediate levels so no zipper noise occurs ([ES9219 datasheet v1.2](https://www.esstech.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/ES9219-Datasheet-v1.2.pdf), Volume Control section, extracted).
- **Analogue attenuation** keeps the converter at full scale so its noise floor drops with the signal. Its cost: potentiometer or resistor-network tolerance, channel mismatch, wiper noise, board area. The measured state of the art is **hybrid**: the ES9219's on-chip **analog volume control** runs +2 dB to -22 dB in 2 dB steps (1 dB steps enableable) "for improved noise performance as the gain is reduced", plus **DRE (dynamic range enhancement)**, which rides the analog gain automatically — DNR 121 dB-A rising to 123 dB-A with DRE on (all from the extracted datasheet). RME's ADI-2 series does the same at box level: digital 0.5 dB steps combined with switched analogue reference levels ("Auto Ref Level"), with measured channel matching around 0.01 dB ([SoundStage measurements](https://www.soundstagenetwork.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=2886), [RME forum on the volume architecture](https://forum.rme-audio.de/viewtopic.php?id=39787) — via search summaries).
- **What measured products do:** the dongles and desk DACs that chart high on Audio Science Review use the DAC chip's own digital volume (plus DRE / analog volume control where the chip has it); none of the well-measured small devices put a potentiometer in the signal path.

### Findings: taper practice

- Established practice is **decibel-linear** control ("audio taper"): perceived loudness varies approximately linearly with the logarithm of signal power, so equal control increments should give equal dB changes. This is documented as the design rationale for audio-tapered volume controls in Microsoft's Core Audio guidance ([Audio-Tapered Volume Controls, Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/coreaudio/audio-tapered-volume-controls) — fetched) and is why logarithmic-taper potentiometers exist ([TI blog on log pots](https://e2e.ti.com/blogs_/archives/b/thesignal/posts/logarithmic-potentiometers)). A roughly 10 dB increase is conventionally a doubling of perceived loudness.
- The USB Audio Class specification expresses volume control values in units of **1/256 dB**, over a range of +127.9961 dB down to -127.9961 dB ([USB Device Class Definition for Audio Devices, Release 1.0, p.71](https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/audio10.pdf) — verified in the fetched PDF). The host-side plumbing already carries far finer resolution than any detent grid.
- Chip-native step size is **0.5 dB** on both leading candidates (above); RME uses 0.5 dB steps over most of its range.

### Assessment: mapping 60 detents

The numbers below are arithmetic on the verified figures above, not sourced claims.

- **Range:** a useful listening range is about 60–70 dB below 2 Vrms full scale. The natural mapping for a 60-detent ring is **1 dB per detent, dB-linear: 0 dB at the top detent, -59 dB at the bottom, mute below the last detent**. One full revolution = 60 dB. Each detent is then a perceptually equal step, and the mapping is exactly the audio-taper practice cited above — with sixty detents there is no need for the compressed-bottom taper a coarse knob forces.
- **Fine mode:** 0.5 dB per detent (two revolutions for the full range) — both chips step natively at 0.5 dB, so no interpolation is needed. The 60's software-defined detent feel could switch dB-per-detent by context (coarse far from target, fine near it) — something no fixed-taper potentiometer can do.
- **Sub-detent rotation:** if the dial reports inter-detent angle, quantise to the chip's 0.5 dB grid and let the chip's soft ramp do the smoothing — the ES9219 explicitly ramps each 0.5 dB step through up to 64 intermediate levels, and the CS43131 has a programmable soft ramp, so a stream of small setpoint updates is zipper-free by construction. Do not synthesise finer-than-0.5 dB steps by rapid register writes; ramp rate is the chip's job.
- **Where to attenuate:** run the chip's digital volume as the working control; leave the ES9219's DRE / analog volume control (or the CS43131's equivalent settings) enabled so the analogue stage tracks down automatically at low listening levels. This reproduces the RME hybrid architecture inside one chip.
- The dial should control the **device's own DAC volume** and absorb or reflect the USB host volume (1/256 dB resolution available on the wire) rather than double-attenuate — one attenuator in the chain, the one closest to the analogue output.

## 5. Evidence from measured products

- **CS43131**: Moondrop Dawn / Dawn Pro (dual CS43131) and Tanchjim Space (dual CS43131) are the archetypal well-measured dongles; Audio Science Review's Tanchjim Space review measured distortion components below -130 dB on the 3.5 mm output, though the full SINAD (signal-to-noise-and-distortion ratio) suite did not complete on that unit ([ASR review](https://www.audiosciencereview.com/forum/index.php?threads/tanchjim-space-portable-headphone-adapter-review.46231/) — fetched). Budget "which CS43131 dongle" threads on the ASR forum treat the chip class as a solved problem ([ASR thread](https://www.audiosciencereview.com/forum/index.php?threads/indecisive-choosing-between-four-budget-cirrus-logic-cs43131-balanced-dongles.63140/)).
- **CS43198 plus separate amplifier**: Truthear SHIO (dual CS43198; maker spec 117 dB SINAD, noise under 1.6 uVrms A-weighted, per [ShenzhenAudio](https://shenzhenaudio.com/blogs/news/truthear-shio-dual-cs43198-dac-amp-is-up)). Note: several reviews mis-report the SHIO as CS43131; the vendor page says CS43198.
- **ES9219 family**: Qudelix 5K — Audio Science Review measured about 93 dB SINAD at 0.4 Vrms and SNR 118 dB (3.5 mm) / 122 dB (2.5 mm), and recommended it ([ASR review](https://www.audiosciencereview.com/forum/index.php?threads/qudelix-5k-bluetooth-dac-headphone-amp.17386/) — figures via search summary). Good, but that Bluetooth-platform implementation lands roughly 20 dB below the best CS43131 dongles.
- **AK4493S**: Topping E30 II (dual AK4493S) — Audio Science Review measured up to about 118 dB SINAD on Toslink input, "near state of the art performance for a DAC selling for $149" ([ASR review](https://www.audiosciencereview.com/forum/index.php?threads/topping-e30-ii-dac-review.36028/) — figures via search summary).
- **ES9038Q2M**: FiiO KA3 dongle (maker spec 122 dB SNR, 0.0008 % THD+N, per [Headfonics](https://headfonics.com/fiio-ka3-review/)) — shows the chip works in a dongle but needs the external op-amp stage done well.
- **Reference floor**: the Apple USB-C dongle (custom Cirrus CS46L06): 98 dB SINAD, 113 dB SNR, 0.9 ohm output impedance, ASR-measured (via search summary of the [ASR thread](https://www.audiosciencereview.com/forum/index.php?threads/apple-dongle-usb-c-to-3-5-mm-adc-quality-with-earpods-free-headsets.34219/)). Whatever the 60 ships must beat this $9 part on the bench or the audiophile positioning fails on measurement.

**Assessment:** the CS43131 has the deepest well-measured implementation record for exactly this use case (USB-powered, one jack, 32–300 ohm); the ES9219's record is via the Qudelix/HiBy class, which measures a tier lower in shipped practice. Chips without integrated drivers (CS43198, AK4493S, ES9038Q2M/ES9039Q2M, PCM1794A) measure superbly only behind a competent discrete stage — more board, more rails, more ways to lose 10 dB.

## 6. Confidence and gaps

**High confidence (primary datasheet or distributor page fetched, or PDF extracted locally):** ES9219 performance, rails, power, charge pumps, volume architecture and ordering table; CS43131 performance, rails, power, output power, current limiter and volume steps; USB Audio Class 1/256 dB volume unit; NwAvGuy 1/8th-rule wording; Microsoft audio-taper guidance; stock and prices for CS43131 (DigiKey, LCSC), CS43198, ES9038Q2M, ES9039Q2M, AK4493SEQ, AK4377A, PCM5102A, PCM1794A, TPA6120A2, OPA1622 and ES9018K2M (LCSC).

**Medium confidence (credible sources seen only in search summaries; primary page not fetched):** Audio Science Review figures for the Qudelix 5K, Topping E30 II and Apple dongle; ES9038Q2M / ES9039Q2M / ES9018K2M headline specs (datasheet blurbs via search); AK4377A output power and package; the Qudelix forum statement that the ES9219C is replaced by the ES9219MQ.

ES9219Q/MQ supply looks thin either way — for a 60-unit run plus spares, get an authorized-distributor quote early. **Quote required** for ES9219Q at quantities 50 and 500.

3. CS43198, ES9038Q2M and ES9039Q2M supply-rail tables were not extracted — verify before any schematic work.
