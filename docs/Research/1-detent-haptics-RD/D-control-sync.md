# Strand D — Control and synchronisation of the passive detent, motor layer and vibration actuator

Research strand for the 60's detent/haptics R&D brief. Date: 2026-09-02. Status: desk research, no bench measurements yet.

**Scope.** How firmware should combine the sixty-pole passive magnetic detent (~60–150 mNm peak, always present) with the driven motor layer and the LRA-class (linear resonant actuator) event layer; what position-sensing resolution, loop rate and latency the combination demands; how end-stops, ramps and half-steps should be rendered; failure modes.

**Method note.** Every factual claim is cited. Where a figure came only from a search-result excerpt and the primary paper could not be opened (paywall or unparseable PDF), it is marked "reported via excerpt, primary source unverified". Everything else labelled "estimate, unverified" is my own derivation.

## 1. Combining a fixed passive detent with a driven layer

### 1.1 The architecture the literature converges on: superposition with feed-forward cancellation

The passive magnet field is, from the controller's point of view, a known, fixed, angle-periodic torque disturbance — exactly analogous to cogging torque in a permanent-magnet motor. The standard treatment is to characterise it once (torque vs angle over one detent period) and apply it as a feed-forward term, so the motor renders only the *difference* between the desired torque profile and the passive profile:

> τ_motor(θ) = τ_desired(θ) − τ_passive(θ)

- When the desired feel *is* the 6° detent (music-volume context), τ_motor ≈ 0 and the motor is silent — zero power, zero noise, zero ripple. This is the whole payoff of the hybrid design.
- When the desired feel differs (half-steps, end-stops, flat spin), the motor drives the residual.

This is not speculative: it is how the field treats superimposed torque sources. Swindells and MacLean's work on rendering knob feel parameterises a knob as superimposed physical terms (detent + friction + inertia) captured by system identification and then re-rendered ([Swindells PhD thesis, UBC SPIN lab](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/spin/node/272); [Designing for Feel: Contrasts between Human and Automated Parametric Capture of Knob Physics](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260623638_Designing_for_Feel_Contrasts_between_Human_and_Automated_Parametric_Capture_of_Knob_Physics)). The "haptic camera" capture step is precisely what our bench rig should do to the magnet ring: sweep it slowly, log torque vs angle, store the profile as a lookup table (LUT) in flash.

Kuchenbecker, Fiene and Niemeyer's event-based haptics is the model for the third layer: superimpose a short high-frequency transient at the moment of a discrete event (contact, detent crossing, end-stop hit) on top of the slower proportional feedback, because real contact produces "an initial high-frequency transient and a slower extended force" ([Improving contact realism through event-based haptic feedback, IEEE TVCG 2006 — publications list](http://haptics.seas.upenn.edu/index.php/research/publications); [Okamura, Cutkosky, Dennerlein, Reality-based models for vibration feedback, IEEE/ASME Trans. Mechatronics 2001](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228947100_Detection_of_torque_vibrations_transmitted_through_a_passively-held_rotary_switch)). Our LRA on the base is that transient channel; the motor should never be asked to render clicks it is too slow for.

### 1.2 Phase alignment between motor-rendered features and magnet positions

Motor-rendered detents, half-steps and end-stop edges must be defined in the *same angular frame* as the magnet wells, with a stored calibration offset between encoder zero and the nearest magnet well centre. Two periodic torque fields of the same 6° period but offset phase sum to a single detent field that is *shifted and weakened* (vector sum of two sinusoids at the same spatial frequency — estimate, unverified but elementary). The felt symptom of a phase error is therefore not two detents but one mushy detent whose centre disagrees with the screen's tick marks — and, because the offset drifts with temperature and reassembly, it must be a calibration constant, not a compile-time constant.

Tan, Yang et al. found that detection of torque variation through a rotary switch "depends on the spatial, not temporal, specification of the torque profiles" ([Manual Detection of Spatial and Temporal Torque Variation through a Rotary Switch, IEEE Trans. Haptics 2008](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4653491/); [Purdue PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~hongtan/pubs/PDFfiles/J38_Tan_etal_ToH2008.pdf)). Consequence: define every rendered feature (detent centres, ramp shapes, wall positions) as functions of angle, never of time, and phase-lock them to the magnet frame.

The proposed six-pole/sixty-tooth variable-reluctance motor geometry shares the 6° period of the passive field by construction, which means motor torque ripple and passive detent torque are at the same spatial frequency and phase-lockable — no beat frequency between the layers. An axial-flux ring stator with a different pole count would put motor ripple at a different spatial period than the detents, producing a beat the fingers can find (see §6). This is a real argument for the variable-reluctance route at the control level, independent of the mechanical arguments.

### 1.3 A rendered half-step between two magnet wells

The midpoint between two 6° wells is an *unstable equilibrium* of the passive field: the magnets push away from it toward either neighbour. To make a stable rendered position there, the motor must supply, around that point, positive stiffness greater than the passive field's negative stiffness, i.e. locally overpower and invert the magnet gradient. Near the midpoint the passive restoring torque is small (it crosses zero there), but a user resting a few tenths of a degree off-centre will be pulled outward with torque rising toward the passive peak (~60–150 mNm at roughly the quarter-period point — estimate of peak location, unverified). Practical consequences:

- The motor's continuous torque rating must comfortably exceed the passive peak, or half-steps will feel like a shallow ledge on the slope of a hill rather than a well. Flag for the bench rig: this, not end-stops, sets the motor torque spec.
- Uncancelled residual (LUT error, temperature drift of magnet strength) appears as asymmetry: the half-step feels closer to one neighbour than the other. Tan et al.'s thresholds (§6) suggest residuals must be held to a few per cent of peak torque to be imperceptible.
- The cheaper alternative the mechanism already provides: lowering the magnet carrier 3 mm at gesture cadence weakens the passive field before half-step contexts engage, cutting the torque the motor must fight. Firmware should treat carrier position as part of the haptic state machine, not an independent feature.

### 1.4 Ramping into a magnet well — the handoff

Three rules, all with precedent:

1. **Ramp in angle, not time** (Tan et al., above): fade the motor's cancellation/addition torque over an angular window (e.g. the outer 30% of the detent period — estimate, unverified) so that whatever the turn speed, the felt profile is identical.
2. **Slew-limit torque changes at mode switches.** When a context change re-targets the profile while the knob is stationary inside a well, torque must ramp over time slowly enough not to feel like a kick. SmartKnob's firmware has an analogous mechanism: after 500 ms idle it drifts the detent centre toward the actual position at rate `IDLE_CORRECTION_RATE_ALPHA = 0.0005` per tick, silently re-registering without felt motion ([motor_task.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scottbez1/smartknob/master/firmware/src/motor_task.cpp)). Adopt the same idea for handoff: when target and passive fields align, decay τ_motor to zero exponentially rather than stepping it.
3. **Go dead inside the well.** SmartKnob clamps a dead zone of 20% of detent width, max 1° (`DEAD_ZONE_DETENT_PERCENT = 0.2`, `DEAD_ZONE_RAD = 1 * _PI / 180`) around each detent centre to prevent hunting/limit-cycling at rest ([motor_task.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scottbez1/smartknob/master/firmware/src/motor_task.cpp)). We are better placed: inside a magnet well the passive field holds position, so the motor can drop to exactly zero torque across the whole well floor — no idle current, no hunt. The dead zone only matters in motor-defined (half-step, weakened-field) modes.

## 2. Position-sensing resolution

### 2.1 What SmartKnob actually uses

SmartKnob uses the MagnTek **MT6701** magnetic angle sensor, read over SSI (synchronous serial interface). The project README calls it an "excellent sensor at a reasonable price - highly recommended", chosen because it is "less noisy than TLV493D, and more responsive (control loop is more stable) using SSI" ([SmartKnob README](https://github.com/scottbez1/smartknob/blob/master/README.md)). The MT6701 is a **14-bit** sensor — 16,384 counts/rev, **0.022°/count** ([SimpleFOC community, MT6701 support thread](https://community.simplefoc.com/t/mt6701-magnetic-position-encoder-support/2618)). Note the stated reason is *noise and loop stability*, not raw resolution: the sensor was upgraded from the noisier TLV493D because velocity estimation, not position display, is the binding constraint.

### 2.2 Derivation for the 60

- 60 detents → 6° pitch. SmartKnob's 14-bit sensor puts **~273 counts across one 6° detent** — enough to define torque profiles within a detent as smooth curves rather than staircases.
- Colgate and Brown identify **displacement sensor quantization and velocity filtering** as two of the four factors limiting the achievable impedance range (Z-width) of a haptic display; velocity derived from a coarse encoder is noisy, and that noise feeds straight into any damping term, destabilising walls ([Factors Affecting the Z-Width of a Haptic Display, ICRA 1994, PDF](https://cim.mcgill.ca/~hayward/Teach/papers/Part-II/Colgate-Brown-94.pdf)).
- Perceptual floor: Tan et al. measured detectable spatial torque ripple down to spatial periods of **2.8°** and amplitude thresholds down to **0.37% of a 30 N·mm mean torque** at the most sensitive spatial frequencies ([Tan et al. 2008, Purdue PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~hongtan/pubs/PDFfiles/J38_Tan_etal_ToH2008.pdf) — figures reported via excerpt, primary source unverified). To render (or cancel) a feature with 2.8° spatial period cleanly you want, as a rule of thumb, at least ~20–50 samples per period → 0.06–0.14° resolution → **2,600–6,400 positions/rev minimum, i.e. 12–13 bits** (rule-of-thumb multiplier is an estimate, unverified).
- Velocity estimation dominates: differentiating position at 1 kHz, quantisation noise in velocity is (resolution in rad) × 1000 rad/s. At 14 bits that is ~0.38 rad/s of raw velocity noise before filtering (arithmetic from the count size — estimate, unverified); at 10 bits it is 16× worse and a damping term becomes unusable. This is why SmartKnob's move to a cleaner 14-bit SSI sensor stabilised its loop.

**Specification: minimum 12-bit (4,096 counts/rev, 0.088°); target 14-bit-equivalent or better (≥16,384 counts/rev, ≤0.022°), with low cycle-to-cycle noise prioritised over absolute accuracy.** The optical path makes this cheap to exceed (§5).

## 3. Control loop rate and latency

### 3.1 What the references actually run

- **SmartKnob:** the motor task loops with `delay(1)` — **~1 kHz torque update** — publishing state every 5 ms; torque is computed as `motor.PID_velocity(input)` with P gain 4× detent strength, endstop gain 4× endstop strength, output clamped at `motor.PID_velocity.limit = 10` ([motor_task.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scottbez1/smartknob/master/firmware/src/motor_task.cpp)).
- **Haptic rendering convention:** ~1 kHz is the widely accepted standard servo rate; the standard explanation is that lower rates make virtual surfaces feel softer or vibratory ([Basdogan & Srinivasan, Haptic Rendering in Virtual Environments, VR handbook chapter, PDF](https://mysite.ku.edu.tr/cbasdogan/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/VRbookChapter.pdf)). Hayward and MacLean's Do-It-Yourself Haptics tutorial covers the same servo-rate ground ([Part I, PDF](https://cim.mcgill.ca/~haptic/pub/VH-KM-RAM-07.pdf) — PDF not machine-readable in this pass; rate figures not directly quoted, unverified).
- **Update rate vs perceived quality:** Choi and Tan showed update rate directly changes perceived instability of rendered haptic texture, and higher update rates permit substantially stiffer "perceptually clean" rendering — an increase of ~278% in maximum clean stiffness is reported between low and high update rates ([Effect of update rate on perceived instability of virtual haptic texture](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4122284_Effect_of_update_rate_on_perceived_instability_of_virtual_haptic_texture); [Perceived Instability of Virtual Haptic Texture III, Presence 2007, PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~hongtan/pubs/PDFfiles/J25_ChoiTan_Presence3_2007.pdf) — the 278% figure reported via excerpt, primary attribution unverified).
- **Automotive:** the Badescu/Wampler/Mavroidis rotary haptic knob (GM/Caltech, Haptics Symposium 2002) rendered menu detents and spring behaviour with 146 mNm motor stall torque plus a 1.1 N·m brake ([Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Rotary-haptic-knob-for-vehicular-instrument-Badescu-Wampler/8e1659229b7e9fa452b2bd8eeea644a6b07385c9)); a related haptic-knob paper reports a 1 kHz real-time control frequency ([Design and control of a haptic knob](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257347416_Design_and_control_of_a_haptic_knob) — 1 kHz figure reported via excerpt, primary source unverified). BMW's iDrive used Immersion-designed haptic profiles ("detents, hard stops, 'hills'") on a force-feedback knob ([Electronic Design on iDrive](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/boards/article/21760094/can-bmws-idrive-pass-its-road-test-now); [Immersion TouchSense rotary modules brochure, PDF](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~jfr/embeddedctrls/files/TSRotary_Oct04_v3_LR.pdf)).

### 3.2 Latency budget

- **Torque loop (solid vs mushy detents):** the loop's sample period *is* the latency that matters for stability; Colgate and Brown's passivity analysis shows achievable virtual stiffness scales inversely with sample time and directly with physical damping ([Colgate & Brown 1994, PDF](https://cim.mcgill.ca/~hayward/Teach/papers/Part-II/Colgate-Brown-94.pdf)). Budget: **encoder read → torque command update ≤ 1 ms (one 1 kHz tick), jitter ≪ 1 tick.** Faster (2–5 kHz) buys stiffer stable walls and cleaner cancellation at flick speeds; at 20 rev/s a flick crosses a 6° detent in 0.8 ms, so 1 kHz is marginal for fast spins and a 5 kHz inner torque loop is the safe target (flick arithmetic is an estimate, unverified).
- **Event layer (LRA confirmations):** psychophysics of tactile event latency: users are sensitive to tactile feedback delay; ~18 ms constant delay with low jitter performed best, satisfaction dropped when delay variation exceeded ~72 ms, and a vibration arriving later than ~30 ms after the triggering touch reads as a missed event ([Kaaresoja, Hoggan, Anttila, INTERACT 2011, PDF](https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/interact/interact2011-2/KaaresojaHA11.pdf); [Kaaresoja PhD thesis, Latency Guidelines for Touchscreen Virtual Button Feedback](https://theses.gla.ac.uk/7075/1/2016kaaresojaphd.pdf); [Immersion whitepaper, UX Impacts of Haptic Latency in Automotive Interfaces](https://www.immersion.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/ux-impact_haptic-latency-in-auto_jul13v1.pdf)). Budget: **event trigger → LRA drive start ≤ 10 ms, hard ceiling 30 ms, jitter the enemy more than mean delay.**

## 4. End-stops, ramps, and half-steps

### 4.1 End-stops: spring + damping inside the passivity envelope, plus an event transient

The classic wall-rendering result: a sampled-data virtual wall (spring K, damper B) is only passive — guaranteed stable against any grip — within a region bounded by the sample time and the device's *physical* damping; Colgate and Brown found "inherent interface damping exerts an overwhelming influence on Z-Width" and that adding physical damping raised both maximum stable stiffness and damping ([Factors Affecting the Z-Width of a Haptic Display, PDF](https://cim.mcgill.ca/~hayward/Teach/papers/Part-II/Colgate-Brown-94.pdf)).

This bites the 60 directly: near-frictionless V-wheels mean almost no inherent damping, and the 180 g aluminium ring is nearly pure inertia. A stiff virtual wall on a low-damping, high-inertia device is the textbook recipe for chatter — the wall bounces the ring back and the loop pumps energy. Mitigations, in order:

1. Keep wall stiffness within the passivity bound for the measured loop rate and inherent damping (bench rig measurement; there is no shortcut).
2. Render the wall as **spring gradient + velocity damping**, with the damping term active only inside the wall (standard practice per Colgate/Brown and the rendering-chapter literature above), and filter velocity as lightly as the encoder noise allows — heavy filtering adds phase lag that eats the stability margin.
3. **Do not chase hardness with stiffness — add an event transient instead.** Kuchenbecker et al. showed a superimposed high-frequency transient at contact makes a surface feel much harder than its stable stiffness alone ([event-based haptics, Penn haptics publications](http://haptics.seas.upenn.edu/index.php/research/publications)). Fire the LRA at wall contact; let the motor spring be only as stiff as passivity allows. SmartKnob's approach is consistent: endstop P gain is only 4× (`config.endstop_strength_unit * 4`) with torque clamped, plus a global cutout if velocity exceeds 60 rad/s ([motor_task.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scottbez1/smartknob/master/firmware/src/motor_task.cpp)).
4. One more asset: park the magnet carrier *up* (full detent strength) in end-stop regions — the passive wells behind the wall act as genuine physical damping/ratcheting the wall can lean on (design suggestion — estimate, unverified).

### 4.2 Resistance ramps toward a limit

Shape the ramp **in the angle domain** (Tan et al.: perception keys on spatial profile, [Purdue PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~hongtan/pubs/PDFfiles/J38_Tan_etal_ToH2008.pdf)) as a monotonic offset torque added on top of the (still phase-locked) detent field, so detents stay countable while the floor tilts. Two perceptual anchors: the ramp increment per detent should exceed the torque JND (Weber fraction ~4–12% for torque, 5–10% for force, per the review in Tan et al. — reported via excerpt) to be readable, and BMW/Immersion's "hill" primitive — a permeable barrier before a boundary ([Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/boards/article/21760094/can-bmws-idrive-pass-its-road-test-now)) — is the right model for "approaching the limit" as distinct from "at the limit".

### 4.3 Half-steps between 6° wells

Covered mechanically in §1.3. Rendering recipe: cancel the passive field over the two adjacent periods (LUT feed-forward), then render a synthetic 3°-pitch detent field; drop the magnet carrier 3 mm first so the field to cancel is minimal. Confirm each half-step crossing with an LRA tick, since the synthetic wells will necessarily be shallower than the magnet wells and the transient carries the "click" identity (event-based haptics, above). Do not attempt half-steps at full magnet strength until the bench rig confirms motor torque headroom over the passive peak.

## 5. What this demands of the optical encoder on the inner wall

The constraint (optical, knob inner wall) is favourable for resolution and awkward for two second-order errors:

- **Resolution: easily sufficient.** A reflective incremental scale on the inner wall sits at a large radius, so even a coarse scale pitch yields fine angles. Example: a 20 µm-pitch scale at Ø110 mm ≈ 17,300 lines/rev before interpolation — ~0.02°/line, already 14-bit-class in raw lines (arithmetic example — estimate, unverified; actual scale pitch and radius to be chosen). This comfortably beats the §2 spec.
- **Interpolation error (sub-divisional error, SDE) is the haptics-relevant error.** When resolution is finer than scale pitch the readhead interpolates, and non-linearity appears as cyclic error within every scale period — Renishaw's glossary treats "sub-divisional error" and "interpolation error" as the same thing ([Renishaw optical encoders glossary](https://www.renishaw.com/en/optical-encoders-glossary--27382)). SDE is a *high spatial frequency* error, so it corrupts the velocity estimate (and thus any damping term) far more than the displayed position. Good readheads make it small — e.g. Renishaw VIONiC specifies SDE typically <±15 nm on the scale ([VIONiC series](https://www.renishaw.com/en/vionic-encoder-series--38973)) — but hobby-class reflective sensors will not; SDE of the chosen readhead must be on the bench-rig checklist, evaluated as *velocity noise at 1 kHz*, not as a position spec.
- **Eccentricity/run-out gives a once-per-rev error** ([Renishaw, The accuracy of rotary encoders](https://www.renishaw.com/en/the-accuracy-of-rotary-encoders--47130)). For haptics this is nearly harmless (it is a slow, smooth 1/rev phase drift), *except* that it shifts the encoder frame relative to the magnet frame as a function of angle — so the phase calibration of §1.2 should be a 60-entry per-well offset table, not a single scalar, absorbing eccentricity and magnet placement error together (design suggestion — estimate, unverified).
- **Incremental scales need an index/reference or a startup homing wiggle** to bind the encoder frame to the magnet frame after power-up; an absolute or index-marked scale avoids a felt homing move (design consideration — estimate, unverified). Flag: this is the one real conflict in the constraint — magnetic absolute chips (MT6701-style) give absolute angle for free, an inner-wall optical incremental scale does not.

## 6. Failure modes: ripple, beats, and what fingers can detect

- **How small must parasitic ripple be?** The best direct data is Tan/Yang et al.: through a passively-held/turned rotary switch, sinusoidal spatial torque variation superimposed on a 30 N·mm mean torque was detectable at thresholds from **0.37% to 26.17%** of the mean depending on spatial period (2.8°–180° range tested); separately, sensitivity to torque *noise* corresponded to **1–3% of peak torque** — notably better than the classical Weber fractions (force 5–10%, torque 4–12%) ([IEEE Trans. Haptics 2008](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4653491/); [Purdue PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~hongtan/pubs/PDFfiles/J38_Tan_etal_ToH2008.pdf); companion threshold work: [Thresholds for Dynamic Changes in a Rotary Switch, EuroHaptics 2003, PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/hirl/projects/papers/switches/C39_Yang_EuroHaptics03.pdf); [Detection of torque vibrations transmitted through a passively-held rotary switch](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228947100_Detection_of_torque_vibrations_transmitted_through_a_passively-held_rotary_switch)). Working number: **hold all parasitic torque ripple below ~1% of the local peak torque** to be safely under threshold (the 1% target as a design margin is an estimate, unverified; the 1–3% detection band is cited).
- **Beats.** If motor ripple and the 60-pole passive field have unequal spatial frequencies, their sum has a slow spatial envelope — some detents stronger, some weaker, repeating around the ring. Given the ~1–3% noise sensitivity above, even small motor ripple at a non-multiple period will be felt as "this knob has good detents and bad detents". The six-pole/sixty-tooth variable-reluctance geometry avoids this by sharing the 6° period exactly (see §1.2); an axial-flux stator must have its cogging/ripple period checked against 6° divisibility before selection.
- **Cancellation residual drift.** Magnet strength varies with temperature (N42 NdFeB reversible coefficient ~−0.1%/°C — estimate, unverified, standard grade data not fetched this pass), so a fixed cancellation LUT slowly develops percent-level residuals; given the 1–3% detection floor this is borderline and argues for a slow adaptive scale factor on the LUT.
- **Quantisation/limit-cycle buzz.** Colgate and Brown list sensor quantisation and velocity filtering among the Z-width limiters ([PDF](https://cim.mcgill.ca/~hayward/Teach/papers/Part-II/Colgate-Brown-94.pdf)); SmartKnob's dead zone and its 60 rad/s velocity cutout are the practical guards ([motor_task.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scottbez1/smartknob/master/firmware/src/motor_task.cpp)). Our zero-torque-inside-the-well policy (§1.4) removes the commonest buzz site entirely.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt a three-layer superposition architecture: passive LUT feed-forward + motor residual + LRA (linear resonant actuator) event transients.** Characterise the passive torque-vs-angle profile on the bench rig ("haptic camera" capture, per Swindells/MacLean — [thesis](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/spin/node/272)), store it in flash, and compute motor torque as desired-minus-passive. Evidence: cogging-compensation practice plus event-based haptics ([Kuchenbecker et al.](http://haptics.seas.upenn.edu/index.php/research/publications)). Confidence: high.
2. **Phase-lock all rendered features to the magnet frame via a per-well 60-entry calibration table**, measured at build and re-trimmable in service; define every feature in angle, not time (Tan et al., [Purdue PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~hongtan/pubs/PDFfiles/J38_Tan_etal_ToH2008.pdf)). Confidence: high on the principle, medium on needing per-well (vs scalar) granularity.
3. **Run the torque loop at ≥1 kHz with ≤1 ms encoder-to-torque latency; design headroom to 5 kHz.** Evidence: SmartKnob's 1 ms loop ([motor_task.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scottbez1/smartknob/master/firmware/src/motor_task.cpp)), the 1 kHz rendering convention ([Basdogan chapter](https://mysite.ku.edu.tr/cbasdogan/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/VRbookChapter.pdf)), update-rate/stiffness results (Choi & Tan), and Colgate/Brown's sample-time-limited stiffness bound. Confidence: high for 1 kHz minimum; medium for the 5 kHz target (flick arithmetic is my estimate).
4. **Specify position sensing at ≥12-bit/rev minimum, target ≤0.022° (14-bit-equivalent, ≥16,384 counts/rev), judged on velocity-estimate noise at the loop rate rather than absolute accuracy.** Evidence: SmartKnob's 14-bit MT6701 chosen expressly for loop stability ([README](https://github.com/scottbez1/smartknob/blob/master/README.md); [14-bit spec](https://community.simplefoc.com/t/mt6701-magnetic-position-encoder-support/2618)); Colgate/Brown on quantisation and velocity filtering. Confidence: high.
5. **On the inner-wall optical encoder: demand a sub-divisional-error (interpolation) spec from the readhead vendor, include an index/absolute reference to bind the encoder frame to the magnet frame at power-up, and absorb eccentricity in the per-well calibration table.** Evidence: [Renishaw glossary](https://www.renishaw.com/en/optical-encoders-glossary--27382) and [rotary accuracy note](https://www.renishaw.com/en/the-accuracy-of-rotary-encoders--47130). Confidence: high on SDE mattering; medium on the homing detail (depends on scale choice, unresolved).
6. **Render end-stops as passivity-bounded spring + in-wall damping, capped torque, over-speed cutout, plus an LRA transient at first contact — never chase wall hardness with stiffness alone.** Evidence: Colgate & Brown ([PDF](https://cim.mcgill.ca/~hayward/Teach/papers/Part-II/Colgate-Brown-94.pdf)), event-based haptics, SmartKnob's 4× endstop gain, torque clamp and 60 rad/s cutout ([motor_task.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scottbez1/smartknob/master/firmware/src/motor_task.cpp)). Confidence: high. Bench rig must measure the actual passivity envelope — the near-frictionless V-wheels make it unusually tight.
7. **Zero motor torque across the well floor in default (magnet-defined) contexts; use SmartKnob-style dead zone (20% of pitch, ≤1°) and idle re-centring only in motor-defined contexts.** Evidence: SmartKnob constants ([motor_task.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scottbez1/smartknob/master/firmware/src/motor_task.cpp)); the passive well replaces the active hold. Confidence: high.
8. **Half-steps: drop the magnet carrier first, cancel the residual passive field, render a synthetic 3° field, and confirm crossings with the LRA; verify on the bench that motor continuous torque exceeds the residual passive peak with margin before promising this feature per-context.** Evidence: §1.3 stability argument; event-based haptics for the click identity. Confidence: medium — hinges on unmeasured motor headroom.
9. **Set the parasitic ripple budget at ≤1% of local peak torque and require the motor's ripple period to divide 6° exactly** (the six-pole/sixty-tooth variable-reluctance geometry satisfies this by construction; audit any axial-flux alternative for it). Evidence: 1–3% torque-noise detection sensitivity and spatial-frequency-dependent thresholds down to 0.37% ([Tan et al. 2008](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4653491/)). Confidence: high on the mechanism, medium on the exact 1% figure (design margin, my estimate).
10. **LRA event budget: trigger-to-vibration ≤10 ms, ceiling 30 ms, minimise jitter above all.** Evidence: Kaaresoja et al.
