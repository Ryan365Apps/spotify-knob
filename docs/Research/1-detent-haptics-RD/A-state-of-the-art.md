# Strand A — State of the art in rotary haptic input devices

Research strand A of the detent and haptics R&D brief for the 60 by Cadrane (the Ø135 mm desk dial with sixty magnetic detent positions per turn, target detent strength 60–150 mNm peak). This document surveys existing rotary input devices: what mechanism produces the detent, how many detents per revolution, published torque, whether the feel survives power-off, what users report, and relevant patents.

Method note: every factual claim carries a source link. Where a figure (torque, detent count) is not published anywhere found, it is marked **not published** rather than estimated. Findings are separated from concluding observations; the observations are in the final section only.

---

## 1. SmartKnob (Scott Bezek, 2022) and derivatives

**Mechanism: motor-simulated.** A brushless direct-current (BLDC) gimbal motor with a hollow shaft and diametric magnet drives the knob under closed-loop torque control; a MagnTek MT6701 magnetic encoder provides position, a TMC6300 motor driver provides up to 1.2 A RMS drive, and firmware synthesises detents and endstops as torque-versus-angle profiles. A 240×240 round liquid-crystal display (LCD, GC9A01) sits inside the hollow shaft. Press detection uses printed-circuit-board (PCB) flexure measured by surface-mount resistors acting as strain gauges. Sources: [SmartKnob GitHub repository](https://github.com/scottbez1/smartknob), [Hackaday in-depth article](https://hackaday.com/2022/06/24/an-in-depth-look-at-the-haptic-smart-knob/).

**Detent count:** software-configurable, arbitrary — "software-defined endstops and virtual detents" ([GitHub](https://github.com/scottbez1/smartknob)). No fixed mechanical count.

**Torque:** not published. The repository gives the driver current limit (1.2 A RMS for the TMC6300) but no measured detent torque ([GitHub](https://github.com/scottbez1/smartknob)).

**Unpowered:** no. Detents vanish without power. Worse, the repository notes that "nearly every off-the-shelf BLDC gimbal motor has moderate to severe cogging" — an unpowered motor knob snaps to parasitic positions of its own; the project's recommended motor (later stocked by SparkFun as part 20441) was chosen specifically for low cogging ([GitHub README](https://github.com/scottbez1/smartknob/blob/master/README.md?plain=1)).

**Reported feel:** Hackaday describes programmable resistance at predetermined positions "simulating physical clicks without mechanical components," with button-press confirmation delivered as "a quick jerk of the motor shaft" ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2022/06/24/an-in-depth-look-at-the-haptic-smart-knob/)). Hackster called it a dial that "can selectively resist motion and provide haptic feedback" ([Hackster](https://www.hackster.io/news/this-clever-dial-can-selectively-resist-motion-and-provide-haptic-feedback-19731148d3d5)). Commercial derivatives exist (e.g. [SeedLabs SmartKnob DevKit v0.1](https://store.seedlabs.it/products/smartknob-devkit-v0-1)); the chief practical weakness reported was sourcing the hollow-shaft motor after it sold out ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2022/06/24/an-in-depth-look-at-the-haptic-smart-knob/)).

**Patents:** none — the design is open source, Apache License 2.0 for software/electronics and Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 for mechanicals ([GitHub](https://github.com/scottbez1/smartknob)).

## 2. Apple Digital Crown (Apple Watch)

**Mechanism: no physical detents; vibration-substituted.** The crown itself rotates smoothly. From Apple Watch Series 4 (2018) onward it is read by an optical encoder — the iFixit teardown shows an internal cylinder carrying micro-scratches tracked by an optical sensor on a ribbon cable ([iFixit Series 4 teardown](https://www.ifixit.com/Teardown/Apple+Watch+Series+4+Teardown/113044)). The "detents" are haptic taps generated by the Taptic Engine — Apple's linear resonant actuator (LRA)-class vibration motor mounted in the watch body, not in the crown ([iFixit on the Taptic Engine](https://www.ifixit.com/News/16768/apple-taptic-engine-haptic-feedback), [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/115562)). The tap is felt at the wrist while the finger on the crown feels only smooth rotation.

**Detent count:** not published. Apple's developer documentation says the system provides "linear haptic detents" as the user rotates, and some controls tie detents to list items scrolling onto screen — i.e. the count is a user-interface variable, not a hardware constant ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/115562)).

**Torque:** not published (rotation resistance is a plain seal/bearing drag; no figure found).

**Unpowered:** no. Powered off, the crown is a free-spinning smooth wheel.

**Reported feel:** 9to5Mac called the Series 4 crown "a big leap," with incremental clicks felt while scrolling ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2018/10/04/apple-watch-series-4-digital-crown-evolution-review/)). The feedback is well-enough integrated that users notice and complain when it stops working ([Apple Community thread](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/8641123)), and Apple exposes a setting to turn crown haptics off ([iDownloadBlog how-to](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2018/10/02/turn-off-digital-crown-haptic-feedback-apple-watch/)).

**Patents:** [US11016587B2, "Watch crown haptic support to provide detent-like operation"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11016587) (Apple Inc., filed 2018-12-26, granted 2021-05-25) — claims simulating mechanical detents by synchronising haptic-actuator waveforms and audio clicks to display animation events, using a stored "detent table" of waveforms and gains for detent types (mechanical, weak, standard, strong). Also [US20170090572A1, "Haptic feedback for rotary inputs"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20170090572A1/en) — haptic output coupled to a rotary input's rotation. Notable: the patent explicitly recruits **audio** to sell the illusion of a mechanical detent.

## 3. Automotive rotary controllers

### BMW iDrive

**Mechanism, generation 1 (E65 7-series, 2001): motor-simulated force feedback.** The controller contains an encoder and a belt drive beneath the knob shell; position changes trigger haptic commands over the controller-area-network (CAN) bus. The haptic profiles were designed by Immersion Corporation and "contain instructions for detents, hard stops, 'hills,' and other touch-sensitive responses" — a speaker-balance screen gets detents, while a "hill" acts as a permeable barrier between screen regions. Alps Electric manufactured the knob assembly ([Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/boards/article/21760094/can-bmws-idrive-pass-its-road-test-now)). The device is the production descendant of the Alps HAPTIC COMMANDER (2001), described by Alps Alpine as "the world's first use of a tactile sensation device in an automobile," built on Immersion force-feedback technology licensed in 2002 ([Alps Alpine HAPTIC history](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/e/info/technology/tec_haptic/haptic), [Alps–Immersion licensing](https://www.alpsalpine.com/eu/news/detail/2016-0805-01/)).

**Mechanism, later generations: fixed mechanical detents.** Owner-forum history records that the force-feedback controller was standard on the E60 5-series, was then removed — one stated reason being that "the software overhead was too much for the current brains in iDrive" — and that BMW moved to a fixed clicking detent feel ([BimmerFest thread on the change](https://www.bimmerfest.com/threads/idrive-changes-haptic-feedback-gone.204398/), [BimmerFest thread on force feedback and menu ends](https://www.bimmerfest.com/threads/idrive-force-feedback-and-end-of-menus.1364259/)). BMW has since announced removing the rotary controller entirely in favour of touch/haptic panels in the iDrive X generation ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/bmws-idrive-knob-killed-off-for-haptic-controls-and-a-wall-to-wall-screen)).

**Detent count:** not published for any generation. **Torque:** not published. **Unpowered:** generation-1 force-feedback knob, no; later fixed-detent knobs, yes.

**Reported feel:** the recurring criticism of the active-haptic generation was that it "frequently provides too much in the wrong place" ([BimmerFest](https://www.bimmerfest.com/threads/idrive-changes-haptic-feedback-gone.204398/)); a BMW M5 forum thread discusses the force feedback specifically at menu boundaries ([M5Board](https://www.m5board.com/threads/idrive-force-feedback.154128/)).

**Patents:** the Immersion knob-haptics family underpinning this class of device includes [US6636197B1, "Haptic feedback effects for control knobs and other interface devices"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6636197B1/en) — interface modes with "the tactile feel of detents" and spring-centred jog-shuttle feel — and [US7327348B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7327348B2/en), claiming force models for a rotary knob including a scrolling list with detents, a jog shuttle, a push-turn model and rate-control borders. Also in this class: [US8138865](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8138865) and its divisional [US8212639](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8212639), which combine a **mechanical** detent mechanism with added haptic feedback on one rotary knob.

### Audi MMI (Multi Media Interface) and Mercedes COMAND (Cockpit Management and Data system)

**Mechanism:** conventional detented mechanical rotary encoders with push function; failure reports point to "faulty rotary encoders or worn internal contacts" as the usual fault mode, confirming a contact/detent encoder rather than force feedback ([JustAnswer troubleshooting, Audi Q7/A6 MMI knob](https://www.justanswer.com/audi/9jiq7-audi-q7-2008-audi-q7-mmi-control-knob-does-not-work-when-rotated.html)). **Detent count:** not published. **Torque:** not published. **Unpowered:** yes (mechanical detents). **Reported feel:** little published on feel per se; the notable user criticism of Audi MMI is mapping, not tactility — the knob "seems to turn in the wrong direction" for the on-screen movement ([AudiWorld thread](https://www.audiworld.com/forums/a6-c7-platform-discussion-194/mmi-control-knob-counter-intuitive-2838753/)). No detailed public source on the Mercedes COMAND controller's internal detent construction was found; **not published**. **Patents:** none specific found for either controller's detent mechanism.

### Porsche PCM (Porsche Communication Management)

**Mechanism:** small mechanical rotary controller ahead of the gear selector; internal construction **not published**. **Reported feel:** CarExpert's review of the PCM system notes the rotary controller exists for menu scrolling but is "realistically… surplus to requirements" now the screen is touch-first ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/porsche-communication-management-pcm-infotainment-review)). Count/torque: not published.

### Genesis (GV80 / GV70) rotary controller

**Mechanism:** mechanical detented rotary controller, knurled metal rim with "crystal-like" glass detailing; internal detent construction **not published**. **Reported feel:** reviewers praise the materials — knurled finishes and buttons with "a heavy, expensive feel," crystal insets adding "expensive-feeling heft" ([Motor Authority](https://www.motorauthority.com/news/1126731_2021-genesis-gv80-price-specs-review-photos-info), [U.S. News interior review](https://cars.usnews.com/cars-trucks/genesis/gv80/2025/interior)) — while criticising the interaction: the GV80 dial is "somewhat clunky to operate" and the flush mounting was hard to grab, which Genesis fixed by raising the dial in the GV70 ([Edmunds pros-and-cons](https://www.edmunds.com/car-news/2021-genesis-gv80-control-knob-pros-and-cons.html), [Out Motorsports GV70 review](https://www.outmotorsports.com/2021/11/2021-genesis-gv70-review/)). Count/torque: not published. Unpowered: yes (mechanical).

## 4. Microsoft Surface Dial

**Mechanism: no mechanical detents; vibration-substituted.** The iFixit teardown found smooth bearing rotation with a rotation sensor (visually similar to the Nest thermostat's) and a pancake vibration motor — an eccentric rotating mass (ERM) type — glued into the midframe with a rubber O-ring for damping; the motor produces "the buzzy feedback you get when spinning or clicking the Dial" ([iFixit teardown](https://www.ifixit.com/Teardown/Microsoft+Surface+Dial+Teardown/74808), [iFixit teardown article](https://www.ifixit.com/News/8601/surface-dial-teardown)).

**Detent count:** none mechanical. In software, the Windows RadialController application-programming-interface delivers a rotation event at a default resolution of 10 degrees of rotation — 36 events per revolution — and "each input event causes the device to vibrate"; applications can change the resolution and disable haptics ([Microsoft Learn, Surface Dial interactions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/uwp/ui-input/windows-wheel-interactions)).

**Torque:** not published.

**Unpowered:** no. Without power it is a smooth free-turning aluminium puck.

**Reported feel:** "As you twirl the gadget there's a subtle vibration under your fingers from the haptics, to give you a satisfying sensation your selections are having an impact" ([Windows Central review](https://www.windowscentral.com/surface-dial-review)); "the turning mechanism is very smooth… marvellously tactile and simply feels good to use" ([The Digital Lifestyle review](https://thedigitallifestyle.com/w/2017/06/microsoft-surface-dial-review-video/)). The vibration confirms motion but offers no resistance profile.

**Patents:** related rotary-haptic art surfaced in the patent search includes [US8797153B2, "Rotary control device with haptic feedback"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8797153B2/en) (haptic generator selecting among predetermined vibrations radiating along the control surface; assignee not verified against Microsoft) and its application [US20120249315A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20120249315A1/en). No Microsoft patent specifically covering the Surface Dial's detent substitution was positively identified — treat that as a gap in this search, not proof of absence.

## 5. Senic Nuimo

**Mechanism: none (smooth ring) plus vibration confirmation.** A "super-smooth aluminum ring with over a thousand unique points along the inner edge" — a high-resolution analogue ring encoder — with capacitive touch, a gesture sensor, a light-emitting-diode (LED) matrix display, and "haptic click" vibration feedback ([SlashGear hands-on](https://www.slashgear.com/senic-nuimo-hands-on-the-iots-tactile-smart-controller-06421795/), [Red Dot Award entry](https://www.red-dot.org/project/nuimo-34340)).

**Detent count:** none. **Torque:** not published. **Unpowered:** no detent feel at any time (deliberately smooth).

**Reported feel:** "the rotate volume ring being smooth and well-weighted," build quality "superb" ([Product Reviews Net](https://www.product-reviews.net/nuimo-review-innovative-smart-home-automation-controller/)); Sound & Vision found it ran "rings around other smart remotes" on design ([Sound & Vision](https://www.soundandvision.com/content/nuimo-runs-rings-around-other-smart-remotes)). The product is discontinued; Senic's own developer notes are on [Medium](https://medium.com/senic-gmbh/developing-for-the-nuimo-controller-7292becfacff).

**Patents:** none found in this search.

## 6. Camera lens rings — Fujifilm and Leica clicked aperture rings

**Mechanism: spring-loaded ball detent.** The classic implementation is "a captive spring-loaded ball bearing which settles into shallow holes in the mating part" ([Camera-wiki, Detents](https://camera-wiki.org/wiki/Detents)). For Leica-class 35 mm lenses specifically: "most lenses for 35mm use a compression spring under a steel ball for the click stop, with the ball being 1/16 inch or smaller and the spring 1/32–3/64" ([Photrio forum](https://www.photrio.com/forum/threads/possible-to-make-an-aperture-ring-stiffer.178818/)). Fujinon XF lenses use the same ball-and-socket scheme; per a Fujifilm support response relayed on the Fuji X forum, "the lens blueprints indicate that different size ball bearings should provide different feel on whole stops versus 1/3 stops," though owners report the distinction is not always perceptible ([FujiX-Forum, 50mm f/2 click feel](https://www.fujix-forum.com/threads/50mm-f-2-aperture-ring-click-feeling-changed.92195/)).

**Detent count:** one click per one-third stop on modern Fujinon XF aperture rings (e.g. the XF 56mm F1.2 R WR, [DustinAbbott review](https://dustinabbott.net/2024/05/fujinon-xf-56mm-f1-2-r-wr-review/)); Leica M lenses use half-stop clicks on many models ([Rangefinderforum](https://rangefinderforum.com/threads/to-1-2-stop-detent-or-not-to-detent.8941/)). These are clicks over a limited arc, not per revolution; the angular pitch is **not published** per lens.

**Torque:** not published. **Unpowered:** yes — fully passive, and the feel is the product's selling point.

**Reported feel:** prized enough that owners retrofit click stops into clickless third-party lenses with a ball, spring and notch strip ([Yukosteel mod, Viltrox 56mm](https://www.yukosteel.com/2020/11/mod-click-stop-aperture-viltrox-56mm.html)); conversely, wear changes the click feel noticeably over time and owners complain ([FujiX-Forum](https://www.fujix-forum.com/threads/50mm-f-2-aperture-ring-click-feeling-changed.92195/)), and factory-fit tolerance of stop positions varies between Leica samples ([Rangefinderforum on stop tolerance](https://rangefinderforum.com/threads/aperture-ring-stop-tolerance-on-leica-m-lenses.152022/)).

**Patents:** none specific found; the mechanism is a century old and generic (see [Camera-wiki](https://camera-wiki.org/wiki/Detents)).

## 7. Watch bezels — dive ratchets, Rolex and Omega clicks

**Mechanism: click spring (leaf spring) against a toothed ring.** The rotating bezel carries "toothing on the underside for the ratchet system and anti-reverse function," engaged by a small click spring seated in the case; the bezel is friction-fit over it ([WatchUSeek, how the Submariner bezel is held in place](https://www.watchuseek.com/threads/how-is-the-rolex-submariner-bezel-held-in-place.5437388/), [WatchUSeek, Submariner bezel clicks](https://www.watchuseek.com/threads/submariner-bezel-clicks.126330/)). Unidirectional (counterclockwise-only) rotation is a diving safety feature: an accidental bump "cannot make the remaining time appear longer than it is" ([Oracle of Time bezel guide](https://oracleoftime.com/beginners-guide-to-the-dive-watch-bezel/)).

**Detent count:** 120 clicks per revolution on the modern Rolex Submariner, introduced with reference 16800; earlier bezels were clickless friction fits ([WatchUSeek vintage bezel thread](https://www.watchuseek.com/threads/vintage-sub-bezel-question.267087/)). 60-click bezels persist on vintage-styled divers ([Oracle of Time](https://oracleoftime.com/beginners-guide-to-the-dive-watch-bezel/)).

**Torque:** not published. **Backlash:** owner-measured back-play on a current Submariner bezel "should be less than 0.3mm or nearly imperceptible" at the rim ([WatchUSeek 2020 Submariner backplay thread](https://www.watchuseek.com/threads/2020-submariner-bezel-backplay.5187493/)).

**Unpowered:** yes — entirely passive, and expected to work for decades.

**Reported feel:** 120-click bezels are described as "smoother and more refined" with "more precise alignment with minute markings"; 60-click bezels give "chunkier, more tactile clicks" preferred by some divers, and are "perfectly adequate for real-world diving, where timing is usually measured in whole minutes" ([Oracle of Time](https://oracleoftime.com/beginners-guide-to-the-dive-watch-bezel/)). Watch forums treat bezel action — click crispness, back-play — as a direct proxy for build quality ([WatchUSeek backplay thread](https://www.watchuseek.com/threads/2020-submariner-bezel-backplay.5187493/)).

**Patents:** no specific Rolex or Omega click-mechanism patent numbers surfaced in this search (the searches returned aftermarket click-spring parts, e.g. [generic Submariner click springs](https://mccawcompany.com/product/generic-rolex-submariner-bezel-click-spring-317-16800/)); a dedicated patent-database pass (Espacenet, assignee Rolex SA / Omega SA, International Patent Classification G04B) is the follow-up if bezel patents matter to the 60. Marked **not found in this pass**, not "none exist."

## 8. Loupedeck, Elgato Stream Deck +, Monogram Creative Console

**Loupedeck CT / Live — mechanism:** mechanical detented rotary encoders. A working editor reports "the Loupedeck CT's main rotary knobs are excellent with detents that you can feel as you turn them" ([Gearspace thread](https://gearspace.com/board/mastering-forum/1419390-loupedeck.html)); the CT adds a large central wheel with an embedded touchscreen ([Jonny Elwyn on editing hardware](https://jonnyelwyn.co.uk/film-and-video-editing/using-the-stream-deck-for-video-editing-and-colour-grading/)). Engadget's Loupedeck Live comparison notes "the Loupedeck's dials feel decent, although their lack of feedback is a little jarring at times" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/loupedeck-live-versus-the-stream-deck-170020781.html)). Detent count and torque: **not published**. Unpowered: yes (mechanical detents).

**Elgato Stream Deck + — mechanism:** four metal detented push-encoders. Dexerto's review: "one of the first things reviewers noticed is that the dials don't all have the same tactile feeling" — unit-to-unit detent variance on one device ([Dexerto review](https://www.dexerto.com/tech/elgato-stream-deck-plus-review-1985295/)). PC Gamer credits its "big chunky buttons and dials" with "excellent tactility" against the Loupedeck Live S ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/elgato-stream-deck-versus-loupedeck-live-s/)). Count/torque: **not published**. Unpowered: yes.

**Monogram Creative Console (formerly Palette Gear) — mechanism:** modular machined-aluminium controls; the Dial module carries three endless push-encoders, and the Orbiter module a pressure-sensitive dome/disc ([DPReview review](https://www.dpreview.com/reviews/5752685942/review-monogram-creative-console-a-refinement-of-our-favorite-modular-editing-hardware/), [PetaPixel launch coverage](https://petapixel.com/2019/09/16/monogram-launches-editing-console-with-more-control-and-orbiter-disc/)). DPReview: the controls "feel very well-built" with machined-aluminium tops; press action has "sufficient resistance to prevent accidental clicks" ([DPReview](https://www.dpreview.com/reviews/5752685942/review-monogram-creative-console-a-refinement-of-our-favorite-modular-editing-hardware/)). Whether the encoders are detented or smooth is **not published**; count/torque **not published**. Unpowered: mechanical feel, yes.

**Patents:** none specific found for these three products' detent mechanisms.

## 9. Pro audio encoders — Elektron, Native Instruments, Ableton Push

**Mechanism:** endless mechanical rotary encoders, bought-in, in both detented and smooth variants. The relevant component class is the Alps Alpine EC11 (11 mm metal-shaft encoder) and its peers — and this class supplies the one broadly **published detent torque figure** in this whole survey: the Alps EC11 30-detent variants specify detent torque of **12±5 mN·m** initial (12±4 mN·m after reflow), 30 detents and 15 pulses per revolution, rated 15,000 cycles ([Alps EC11 datasheet via Mouser](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/15/EC11-1370808.pdf), [Alps EC11E15204A3 product page](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/e/products/detail/EC11E15204A3/), [Mouser EC11 30-detent listing](https://www.mouser.com/en/c/passive-components/?m=Alps+Alpine&number+of+detents=30+Detent&series=EC11)).

**Detent count:** 30 per revolution is the common detented-encoder standard in this class ([Alps catalogue](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/assets/catalog/product-catalog-ec-all.en.pdf)); smooth (zero-detent) variants are used where continuous feel is wanted — Elektron's Analog Rytm MK1 encoders are sold as replacement parts explicitly "without detents" ([eBay part listing, "OHNE RASTEN" — German for "without detents"](https://www.ebay.de/itm/276799202225)).

**Unpowered:** yes for the detented variants (mechanical).

**Reported feel:** musicians consistently prefer smooth encoders for continuous parameters: on Elektron's Machinedrum, "endless encoders that aren't detented… feel much better"; "a smooth knob feels better to hands than a detented one and is easier to turn back and forth, while detented knobs make a zipping noise when turned" ([Ableton Forum, detented encoder thread](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?t=87695)). Ableton Push 2 encoders are rate-sensitive (slow = precise, fast = coarse) but "vary greatly in tightness" between units ([Ableton Forum, Push 2 encoder tightness](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?t=220830)); Push 3 encoders are described as "phenomenal — buttery-smooth, not loose at all, with perfect resistance" ([Ableton Forum](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?t=223094); component sourcing discussed on [Elektronauts](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/ableton-push-3-encoder-type-brand/211228)). Native Instruments hardware uses the same bought-in encoder classes; no NI-specific torque figure is published (**not published**).

**Patents:** none specific — commodity encoder mechanisms.

## 10. Griffin PowerMate

**Mechanism: none.** A machined-aluminium knob (released 2001) that rotates, presses, and press-rotates, with a programmable LED underneath; rotation is "smooth and fluid, with no detents or end stops… almost no resistance while turning however it doesn't free spin" ([Fstoppers review](https://fstoppers.com/reviews/reviews-griffin-powermate-8337), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_PowerMate), [Graves on SOHO Technology](https://www.mgraves.org/2010/06/the-griffin-powermate-is-a-great-gadget/)).

**Detent count:** zero. **Torque:** not published (reviewers describe light drag only). **Unpowered:** no detents to lose.

**Reported feel:** "Build quality is top notch. It is actual machined aluminum, not plated plastic" ([llllllll.co big-knob thread](https://llllllll.co/t/big-knob-controllers/20047)); radio hobbyists using it as a tuning knob wished for **more** resistance to match vintage receiver tuning weights ([SWLing Post](https://swling.com/blog/2015/08/hamfest-find-griffin-technology-powermate-controller/)).

**Patents:** none found in this search.

## 11. Cross-cutting patent notes — magnetic detents

Directly relevant to the 60's passive magnet ring (six stationary N42-grade neodymium magnets facing sixty steel poles across a ~0.8 mm radial gap), prior art on passive magnetic detents exists and is old:

- [US3934216A, "Magnetic detent device"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3934216A/en) (1976) — stator and rotor with magnets and shaped pole pieces; relative angular position steers flux paths between selected pole pieces to create detent positions. This is structurally the closest prior art to the 60's magnets-versus-steel-poles architecture.
- [US20020125977A1, "Alternating pole magnetic detent"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20020125977) — flexible bonded sheet magnet contoured onto a rotary knob, pole alignment arranged to maximise detent strength.
- [US6971143B2, "Magnetic detent for rotatable knob"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6971143B2/en) (granted 2005) — sealed enclosures each holding a magnet and a magnetically attractable member, providing tactile/audible steps on a medical-device knob.
- [US20070267283A1, "Detent mechanism"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20070267283A1/en) — a further detent-mechanism filing in the same class.
- [US8138865](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8138865) / [US8212639](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8212639) — hybrid: a mechanical detent mechanism plus active haptic feedback on the same rotary knob, i.e. the layered passive-plus-active idea already exists in patent form.

All of the above are expired or aged filings (a United States utility patent lasts 20 years from filing); a freedom-to-operate check is a separate exercise, but the age of US3934216A (1976) suggests the basic magnet-pole detent is long in the public domain. That is an observation, not legal advice.

## Patterns relevant to the 60's design

These are concluding observations, separate from the findings above; each is tied to cited findings.

**1. There are three families, and only two of them feel like detents.** (a) Passive energy wells — ball-and-spring aperture rings, watch-bezel click springs, detented encoders, magnetic detents — resist and then release; (b) motor-simulated torque profiles — SmartKnob, iDrive generation 1, Alps HAPTIC COMMANDER — also resist and release; (c) vibration substitutes — Surface Dial, Apple Digital Crown, Nuimo — only confirm, never resist. Reviewers describe family (c) with words like "subtle" and "satisfying" ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/surface-dial-review)) but family (c) devices feel smooth under the finger by their makers' own description ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/uwp/ui-input/windows-wheel-interactions), [Apple patent US11016587B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11016587)). The 60's three-layer architecture (passive magnets for the fixed scale, motor for dynamics, linear-resonant-actuator-class actuator for events) maps one layer onto each family — no surveyed device does all three.

**2. Motor-only detents have already failed commercially, in exactly the way the 60's settled architecture avoids.** BMW shipped pure force-feedback detents in 2001 and retreated to fixed mechanical detents, with users saying the haptics were "too much in the wrong place" and the software overhead too high ([BimmerFest](https://www.bimmerfest.com/threads/idrive-changes-haptic-feedback-gone.204398/), [Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/boards/article/21760094/can-bmws-idrive-pass-its-road-test-now)). SmartKnob's detents disappear unpowered and its motor cogs parasitically when off ([GitHub README](https://github.com/scottbez1/smartknob/blob/master/README.md?plain=1)). The settled decision that the fixed sixty-position scale is passive-magnetic and the motor only adds dynamics is the corrected version of both failures.

**3. Consistency is what separates premium from cheap — more than strength.** The worst review findings in this survey are all variance findings: Stream Deck + dials that "don't all have the same tactile feeling" on one unit ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/tech/elgato-stream-deck-plus-review-1985295/)); Push 2 encoders that "vary greatly in tightness" ([Ableton Forum](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?t=220830)); Fuji aperture clicks that change feel with wear ([FujiX-Forum](https://www.fujix-forum.com/threads/50mm-f-2-aperture-ring-click-feeling-changed.92195/)); Leica stop positions that vary sample to sample ([Rangefinderforum](https://rangefinderforum.com/threads/aperture-ring-stop-tolerance-on-leica-m-lenses.152022/)). Conversely the premium benchmark, the Rolex Submariner bezel, is judged by back-play under 0.3 mm at the rim ([WatchUSeek](https://www.watchuseek.com/threads/2020-submariner-bezel-backplay.5187493/)). For the 60 this means detent-to-detent torque uniformity (magnet and pole tolerance, gap concentricity across the ~0.8 mm radial gap) and rim backlash are the measurable quality targets, and a magnetic detent's freedom from contact wear ([US3934216A-style architecture](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3934216A/en)) is an advantage no ball-and-spring device in this survey has.

**4. The 60's torque target is far above the desktop norm, and the lever arm is why that is right.** The only published detent torque found is the Alps EC11 encoder class at 12±5 mN·m on a ~6 mm knob shaft ([Alps datasheet](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/15/EC11-1370808.pdf)). The 60 targets 60–150 mNm at a Ø135 mm rim — 5 to 12 times the torque, but applied at roughly ten times the radius, so the fingertip force per detent lands in a similar-to-somewhat-firmer band than a good encoder click. (Observation, not a sourced equivalence; the bench rig measures the real answer.)

**5. Sixty detents per revolution sits in proven territory, on the "tactile" side of it.** Watch bezels ship at 60 and 120 clicks per revolution; 60-click bezels are characterised as "chunkier, more tactile," 120-click as "smoother, more refined" ([Oracle of Time](https://oracleoftime.com/beginners-guide-to-the-dive-watch-bezel/)). Detented encoders standardise on 30 per revolution ([Alps catalogue](https://tech.alpsalpine.com/assets/catalog/product-catalog-ec-all.en.pdf)). Sixty positions on a Ø135 rim gives ~7 mm of rim travel per detent — more travel per click than a 120-click Ø40 mm watch bezel — so the 60 can afford watch-bezel crispness without feeling busy. (The 7 mm figure is arithmetic from the settled geometry, not a measurement.)

**6. Smoothness is a feature when the parameter is continuous — and the audio world says so consistently.** Musicians prefer non-detented encoders for continuous parameters because detents fight fine adjustment and make "a zipping noise" ([Ableton Forum](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?t=87695)); Griffin PowerMate and Nuimo were both praised specifically for smooth, weighted rotation ([Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/reviews/reviews-griffin-powermate-8337), [Product Reviews Net](https://www.product-reviews.net/nuimo-review-innovative-smart-home-automation-controller/)). The 60's motor layer is the only surveyed-architecture answer to wanting both: it can null the magnetic detents for seek/scrub contexts — but note that nulling a passive 60–150 mNm detent field continuously is a motor duty no surveyed device demonstrates; that is a bench-rig question, not a solved problem.

**7. Mass and material carry a large share of perceived quality even with zero detents.** PowerMate ("actual machined aluminum," [llllllll.co](https://llllllll.co/t/big-knob-controllers/20047)), Surface Dial (aluminium, "marvellously tactile," [The Digital Lifestyle](https://thedigitallifestyle.com/w/2017/06/microsoft-surface-dial-review-video/)), and Genesis's knurled, crystal-inset dial ("heavy, expensive feel," [Motor Authority](https://www.motorauthority.com/news/1126731_2021-genesis-gv80-price-specs-review-photos-info)) all earn their reviews substantially from mass and machining. The 60's heavy knurled ring is aligned with this; the Genesis finding also warns that material quality does not rescue a confusing interaction model ([Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/car-news/2021-genesis-gv80-control-knob-pros-and-cons.html)).

**8. Audio is part of the detent illusion — and part of the real thing.** Apple's patent US11016587B2 (the watch-crown detent-simulation patent) explicitly synchronises click **sounds** with haptic waveforms to sell the detent ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11016587)); mechanical detents make their own click, and musicians' complaint about encoder "zipping noise" ([Ableton Forum](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?t=87695)) shows sound is perceived as part of the feel in both directions. The acoustic signature of the 60's magnet-pole engagement (and of the actuator events) deserves deliberate tuning on the bench rig, not accident.
