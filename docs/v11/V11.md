# the 60 — version 11

Built 6 September 2026. v11 is v10 (5 September, `docs/v10/`) with three rulings of 6 September: **the knob's top is a flat rim of at least 20 mm** — and the knob keeps v10's shape, a 4.5 mm wall under a 3 mm lip, so the whole object scales out to the rim: **Ø175.4 outside, bore Ø166.4, structure wall r 81.2–82.8, everything inside re-derived from that** — and **the base plate becomes the heatsink** (`the60-thermal-plan.md` section 14: a black-anodised aluminium core with a ducted underside, a steel rim ring, a closing plate, ribs on top, the converter on the plate, a rear exhaust through the pad). Every bought part is placed as its own CAD body and every part without a body is a named `_ENVELOPE`. 0 check failures in both clutch states; 13 A3 sheets; 10 printed parts + the three-piece plate. Only the internal structure needs print supports (two hidden rings).

## What v11 changes against v10, and why

1. **The rim, and everything scaling out to it** (Ryan, 6 Sep: a flat rim of at least 20 mm on the knob's top; the knob "relatively similar in shape to the prior version, just with a larger rim"; "everything should have got relatively bigger and constants reassessed"). In v10 the bore was set by the panel's chin (r 72.05) and every internal radius was derived from that bore. Now the outside is set by the rim (lip edge r 64.2 + 1.0 chamfer + 20.0 flat + 2.5 chamfer = r 87.7) and the bore by the knob's 4.5 mm wall (r 83.2, Ø166.4); the chin is 11 mm inside it. Derived from the bore, as v10's rules did: structure wall r 81.2–82.8 (0.4 inside the bore), wheel posts r 76.9, pillars r 77.2, deck Ø161, motor bell out to the bore at r 65.7 (ratio 4.75, the bell 9 mm further from the Pi), LED strip on the wall at r 84–85.6 with the diffuser 0.2 in front at the edge, port face at the wall's inner face r 79.7–81.2 with its screw rail inside the wall. The seat flange is a 21 mm shelf (r 62–82.8) — the glass still bonds at r 62.5–65.6. The knob is v10's shape with a 19 mm lip: ≈ 72 cm³, 90 g in PETG (v10: 64 g), inertia ≈ 1.7× v10's.
2. **The base plate is the heatsink** (`the60-thermal-plan.md` section 14, applied by its rules; Ryan's 6 Sep decisions: steel rim ring, converter onto the plate, rear exhaust through the pad). Three pieces. The **aluminium core** (r < 75.2, 6082, black hard anodised on every face including the internal ones, masked bare pads at the ground bond and under the converter): a 4 mm web, a 3 mm duct closed by a **1 mm closing plate** flush in a recess (six M2.5 countersunk screws, all in solid web — unscrew it to clean the channels), 2 × 6 mm ribs on the top face at r 50–70 in the free crescents. The **steel rim ring** (r 75.2–87.7, solid 8 mm — the plug bodies and the halo need the height at the edge, nowhere else) carries the engine-turned edge, the chamfers, the structure's three plate screws, the port face's two M2 holes, and an inward flange (r 72.2–75.2, z 4–8) that sits in a rebate on the core with seven M3 countersunk screws from below. The rim's inner radius is derived (rule 4): 3 mm inboard of the port face's holes at r 78.2. **The duct**: air enters through a 1.2 mm undercut on the bottom outer edge over the front and sides (az 45–315, a 496 mm² mouth), then through 22 radial grooves 6 × 2 in the ring's underside (the pad closes them; 264 mm²), into a collector channel at r 66–70.2, through 20 straight fin channels 3 × 3 on a 4 mm pitch (ten either side of the Pi window, front to rear), into a rear plenum either side of the port slot, and out through six exhaust grooves at az 319–333 and 27–41 (72 mm²) that open downward where the pad is cut away (rule 10: rear, downward, on a different arc from the intake, not the socket slot). It is one connected volume of 25 cm³ (checked). A **blower envelope 28 × 18 × 5** is reserved in the main duct under the speaker at az 277 — the part is not fitted (rule 12). Honest limits: the motor's through-hole and the Pi window leave no 30 × 30 spot anywhere in the duct, so the envelope is 28 × 18 — a slim blower, or a 20 × 20 × 6 one; the two intake grooves that would land on the motor's hole are omitted; and the channel that links the +y rear quadrant to the front is only 2.25 mm wide between the Pi window's wall and the carriage tab slot's wall.
3. **The converter moves off the deck onto the plate** (rule 15): the 38 × 25 × 8 module stands on a 0.5 mm gap pad in the front crescent at az 172, r 47–72, two M2.5 screws into the web. The second-largest heat source now conducts into cooled metal; the deck's motion board stays where it is (its driver dissipates little at a few hundred milliamps) and the Pi keeps its heatsink.
4. **The pad is a ring** under the rim ring (r 75–84.7), cut away at the exhaust arcs and the port slot. The Pi no longer stands on it — it hangs from the deck's legs, as it always did structurally — and the carriage's shoe rests on the ring's inner edge at 90°.
5. **Rule 14's lid cannot be built in v11.** The deck's boards reach z 29.4 and the panel's back components start at 29.4; there is no gap for a lid inside r 62. In v11 the bonded glass and the seat flange are the cavity's top; the separate lid arrives in production when the deck goes and the boards mount to the plate (rule 15).
6. **Two v10 faults fixed on the way.** The port face's ears sat over the slot's void, fixed to nothing — it now has a rail along its top with two M2 screws into tapped holes in the rim ring. And the 3.5 mm jack's board stood 0.6 mm above the plate top under the wall and the LED strip; the checker had allowed it because a rule meant for "jack parts against the jack board" matched any pair containing `jack_board` (the same hole existed for the USB-C and encoder boards; all three closed). The jack is turned over onto a 16 × 12 board with its pins down into the pad's void.
7. **Mass** (rule 16): aluminium core ≈ 143 g + steel rim ring ≈ 333 g + closing plate ≈ 24 g = **≈ 500 g**, against 660 g for v10's Ø154 steel plate and ≈ 282 g had the ring been aluminium. The steel ring puts 333 g at the largest radius, which is where it does most for heft.

Carried unchanged from v10 (see `docs/v10/V10.md` for the reasoning): the display bonded to the seat; the Active Cooler's fan off; the servo on the deck pushing the tall carriage tab; the Pi's orientation and the deck layout; the LED strip on the wall; the vent slits (acoustic only, per the thermal plan — they are not in the cooling path), the open-topped windows and the print review (seat flange cut away above the wheels, motor window open to the seat, the encoder tower's open crush-ribbed slot, captive M2.5 nuts in the deck's top, port face printed face-down, full-height cradle fingers, knurl run-out through the bottom chamfer); the engine-turned edge; the grouped assembly STEP.

## Bench items that decide whether v11 survives

1. **The Pi 5 without its fan at ~4 W in the closed cavity**: log the SoC temperature for an hour of the real workload. If it throttles, the answer is option B (Pi inverted on a plate boss), not a bigger hole.
2. **The adapter's panel connector** must mate with the 45-way 0.3 mm flex (question 17) and its HDMI socket must be on the edge the model assumes (its −x edge as placed, facing the ribbon).
3. **The lens**: Ø140 × 2.5 with the touch tail at the flex edge — anything else changes the seat, the nubs and the lip overlap.
4. **Motor base and bell** as v9 (base ≤ Ø31, bell from ≥ 4.3 above its base).
5. **The 1.9 mm diffuser** 0.2 in front of the strip — opal grade; and the strip itself (width, LED pitch, adhesive) — the model assumes 5 × 1.6.
6. **Halo current**: 3.2 A at full white; the converter module must be a real 10 A part.
7. **The Ø175.4 knob's inertia** (≈ 1.7× the Ø154 build): the detent torque, the free-spin coast and the servo clutch's release need re-tuning on the bench.
8. **The thermal plan's three measurements** (its section 12): the real sustained load at the 12 V inlet over an hour; the passive baseline on the v11 print with thermocouples on the plate, the knob and in the cavity; and the ducted case with a bought blower taped into the reserved envelope — that measurement decides whether the blower is fitted.
## Printing (`src/printcheck.py`: every part in its print orientation — downward faces, bridges, sideways holes)

Rules applied, in the usual FDM (fused-filament) terms: a face is self-supporting when it is steeper than 45° from horizontal; a flat downward face prints as a bridge when both ends rest on material and the span is short (everything here is under 13 mm); a hole with a sideways axis sags at its top, so holes are printed vertical; walls are two perimeters or more (nothing here is under 1.0 mm — the crush rib is 0.6 and is meant to squash); blind holes end in a 45° point; the face that has to be flat and smooth goes on the bed; chamfers, not fillets, on bed edges (the plate is steel, the knob's bed edge is the 2.5 × 45° chamfer). Sources: [Prusa, Modeling with 3D printing in mind](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135) (45–60° overhangs; 0.45 / 0.9 / 1.35 mm for 1 / 2 / 3 perimeters; 0.3 mm clearance for moving fits; bed faces are the smooth ones; chamfer rather than fillet on the bed); [Rahix, Design for 3D-Printing](https://blog.rahix.de/design-for-3d-printing/) (teardrop or vertical holes; ±0.1 mm per surface; clearance ≥ 2 × the printer's tolerance); [UltiMaker, Design for FFF](https://ultimaker.com/learn/design-for-fff-3d-printing-maximize-your-success/).
